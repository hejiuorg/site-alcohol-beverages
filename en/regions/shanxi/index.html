Shanxi

A core region for light-aroma baijiu in northern China, centred on Xinghua Village in Fenyang, Lvliang; local producers use sorghum, barley and peas with twice-steamed clean processing, separated earthen-jar fermentation and solid-state distillation, giving ethyl acetate-led aromas; the current quality standard is GB/T 10781.2-2022, and in 2025 Fenyang released a core-region group standard and public mark for daqu light-aroma baijiu.

Shanxi is a core region for light-aroma baijiu in northern China, with its industrial centre at Xinghua Village in Fenyang, Lvliang. Local producers use sorghum as the main grain and barley and peas for qu, then apply twice-steamed clean processing, separated earthen-jar fermentation and solid-state distillation. The style is led by ethyl acetate and aims for a clean, fresh, crisp profile. Shanxi's baijiu map is not only Fenjiu, but Xinghua Village and Fenjiu are the starting point for understanding light-aroma technique.

The geographic coordinates of light aroma

Shanxi is a province-level node under China. Baijiu is its most representative distilled drink and belongs to the broader category of baijiu; Fenyang, where Xinghua Village is located, is a core region for light-aroma baijiu. Light aroma is not a brand or a quality tier but a process and flavour type defined by qu, fermentation vessel and the way grain is steamed and distilled.

Shanxi also has other aroma types and local distilleries, but light aroma has the highest industrial concentration. A production area and an administrative boundary are not the same: Fenyang and Xinghua Village are the core origin, while Lvliang and other counties may also produce light-aroma or related styles. “Xinghua Village” on a label points to a specific origin and brand tradition, and not every Shanxi baijiu should be described as Xinghua Village liquor.

Xinghua Village: geography and water

Xinghua Village lies in the foothills of the Lvliang Mountains and the Fen River valley, with higher ground to the north-west and lower ground to the south-east. The four seasons are distinct, and the day-night temperature range suits sorghum and grain for qu. Fenjiu company material states that brewing water comes from deep karst groundwater at 840 m below Xinghua Village, slightly alkaline and suited to light-aroma fermentation. A stable water source matters for earthen-jar fermentation and qu making, but water is only one condition; grain, qu and operation matter just as much.

Fenyang lies in the Fen River valley on the eastern side of the Lvliang Mountains, with a mild climate and four distinct seasons. Climate, soil and water form the natural base of the region, but “geography makes good wine” is an oversimplification. The real differences come from qu type, jar temperature, fermentation cycle and the cuts made during distillation, which turn the same grain and water into different aromas.

Steaming twice and separated jar fermentation

The classic description of light-aroma process is “twice-steamed clean processing, solid-state separated earthen-jar fermentation”. Raw grain and auxiliary material are steamed separately to reduce off-flavours; fermentation and distillation are carried out in two rounds, and the fermenting grain never touches pit mud. The vessel is a ceramic jar set level with the ground, whose wall separates the grain from soil and prevents the pit-mud aromas of strong-aroma baijiu. The jar smooths temperature change and makes the microbial source depend more on qu and the working environment.

In the Fenyang daqu light-aroma route, sorghum and husk are steamed separately, fermented in jars, distilled, then fermented and distilled a second time. Jar fermentation is a specific form of solid-state fermentation; temperature, moisture, qu quantity and loading time determine yield and aroma, while distillation cuts the heads and tails that affect cleanliness and body. Only careful execution gives the “clean to the end” style.

Qu and raw materials

Light aroma uses sorghum as the main grain and barley with peas for qu. Fenjiu material states that its daqu is made from barley, peas and deep karst well water, using three types of qu — clean-grain qu, red-heart qu and after-fire qu — in a 3:3:4 ratio. The three qu types differ in microbial community and enzyme activity, and blending them stabilises saccharification and fermentation while giving a fuller qu aroma.

Qu making is the critical start of light aroma. The temperature curve, turning rhythm and storage time affect the balance of moulds, yeasts and bacteria; poor qu cannot be rescued by careful fermentation. Sorghum starch structure, tannin and protein affect yield and flavour, and grinding degree, wetting temperature and steaming time must match. Good raw material is necessary but not sufficient.

Aroma, standard and Fenjiu

Light aroma is dominated by ethyl acetate, often with green apple, floral and grain notes, a fresh entry, a clean body and a relatively short finish. Compared with the pit aroma of strong aroma and the toasted aroma of sauce aroma, light aroma stresses purity and crispness rather than complexity. Aroma intensity is not quality: the difficulty of light aroma is cleanliness, with few off-flavours, correct aroma and a body that is not hollow.

The current quality standard is GB/T 10781.2-2022 Quality requirements for baijiu, Part 2: Light-aroma baijiu, published on 11 July 2022 and implemented on 1 February 2023; Shanxi Xinghua Village Fenjiu Factory Co., Ltd. is one of the main drafting units. The standard describes the aroma type through sensory, physicochemical and hygiene indicators and sets labelling and testing methods. Fenjiu Group is the representative producer at Xinghua Village, and products such as Qinghua Fenjiu carry light aroma, sorghum, barley and peas on the label. Brand and aroma type must be separated: Fenjiu is a brand, light aroma is a category, and other distilleries may also make light-aroma baijiu to the standard.

Regional identity and geographical indication

In September 2025, Fenyang released a group standard and public mark for the Fenyang daqu light-aroma core region. The standard covers raw materials, production process, grade classification, quality requirements and inspection rules, and the Xinghua Village Fenjiu specialised town also launched a logo and cultural IP. A group standard is a tool for regional self-regulation and quality improvement, not a replacement for a national standard; the public mark helps buyers recognise regional products but cannot be detached from a specific company and batch.

Shanxi also runs a geographical indication protection programme, and time-honoured brands such as Fenjiu are protected through trademarks and intellectual property. Geographical indication, group standard, company brand and product trademark are different layers: a geographical indication stresses origin and specific quality, a group standard stresses regional process and quality requirements, and a brand or trademark stresses company identity. When reading a label, check origin, aroma type, standard number, producer and alcohol content separately rather than relying on the words “Xinghua Village” alone.

Five misunderstandings about the light-aroma region

First, that Shanxi has only Fenjiu. Other light-aroma and local distilleries exist, and brand is not the same as region.

Second, that light aroma means low strength or blandness. Light-aroma baijiu can be strong and can have clear grain aroma and body.

Third, that jar fermentation is only a different container. The jar separates grain from soil microbes and directly changes the aroma profile.

Fourth, that twice-steamed clean processing means two distillations. It refers to separate steaming of grain and auxiliary material plus two full fermentation and distillation rounds, not simple repetition.

Fifth, that a group standard can replace a national standard. A group standard is a regional self-regulation tool, while the national standard remains the floor for quality and labelling.
