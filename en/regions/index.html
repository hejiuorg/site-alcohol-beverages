Aconcagua Valley

The Valle del Aconcagua sub-region of Chile's Aconcagua region, covering the provinces of San Felipe de Aconcagua, Los Andes and Quillota, with legal areas including Panquehue, Quillota and Hijuelas. On Wines of Chile's variety figures the main varieties total about 1,500 hectares, led by Cabernet Sauvignon, Carmenère, Sauvignon Blanc and Syrah. The Aconcagua River runs from the Andes to the Pacific, with a warm inland valley and a large day-night range, and a cooler coastal fringe. Soils are mainly alluvial and colluvial. Reserva is not a quality grade.

Alentejo

A large region in south-central Portugal with a hot, dry climate and soils from granite to schist and clay; led by Aragonez (Tempranillo), Trincadeira and Alicante Bouschet, it is one of Portugal’s most overlooked and best-value red regions.

Alsace

A wine region in north-eastern France, east of the Vosges and on the west bank of the Rhine, known for Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat, mostly labelled by variety and with Grand Cru, late-harvest (VT) and botrytised selection (SGN) tiers.

Andong

A traditional soju region in Andong, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea, made from rice and distilled in a traditional pot still; Andong Soju is designated Gyeongsangbuk-do Intangible Cultural Heritage No. 12, high in alcohol and rich in style, a representative of Korean traditional drink.

Anhui

A province in eastern China crossed by the Yangtze and Huai rivers; northern Anhui is known for strong-aroma and mixed-aroma baijiu, and Gujing Town in Bozhou is the home of Gujing Gongjiu.

Argentina

South America’s second-largest wine producer, with regions from the high-altitude deserts of Mendoza to the cool valleys of Patagonia; Malbec is the international signature, with Torrontés, Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and sparkling wine also important.

Armagnac

A grape brandy region in Gascony, France, known for continuous distillation, single-estate bottlings and vintage releases; Bas-Armagnac, Ténarèze and Haut-Armagnac each show different styles.

Australia

A major wine-producing country in the southern hemisphere, centred on regions in the south-east and south-west; Shiraz, Riesling and Chardonnay show distinctive styles, and appellations use the GI system.

Baden

The appellation of south-west Germany, running about 400 kilometres from Tauberfranken along the Rhine plain to Lake Constance and covering about 15,100 hectares in 2025, the third largest wine region in Germany, in nine sub-zones; volcanic rock in the Kaiserstuhl, limestone in Breisgau and Tuniberg, gneiss in Markgräflerland and glacial gravel by Lake Constance create varied soils, Spätburgunder is the leading red variety and Gutedel is the local speciality of Markgräflerland.

Barbados

A Caribbean island country and a major rum region, where molasses, blended pot and column distillation and tropical ageing produce a more balanced style; place names are not a grade.

Barolo

The Barolo DOCG in the Langhe hills of Cuneo province, Piedmont, promoted to DOCG by presidential decree on 1 July 1980 and made purely from Nebbiolo; its zone spans 11 municipalities including Barolo, Castiglione Falletto and Serralunga d'Alba, with vineyards at 170 to 540 metres, minimum density of 3,500 vines per hectare, maximum yield of 8 tonnes per hectare, minimum natural alcohol of 12.5%, 18 months in wood and 38 months total ageing, 62 for Riserva.

Barossa

Wine region north-east of Adelaide, South Australia, and a GI zone containing the Barossa Valley and Eden Valley regions. Barossa Valley GI covers 578 sq km with 11,609 hectares at 112-596 metres, Shiraz about half the plantings; Eden Valley covers 591 sq km with 2,169 hectares at 219-632 metres, known for Riesling and Shiraz. Mediterranean climate, warm on the valley floor and cooler in the hills. Shiraz was planted by 1843, and the 2009 Barossa Old Vine Charter sets tiers at 35, 70, 100 and 125 years, a voluntary label not a quality grade.

Bavaria

A federal state in southern Germany and one of the world's most famous beer-law territories. The 1516 Reinheitsgebot limited bottom-fermented beer to water, malt, hops and yeast, but modern law leaves room for top-fermented and imported beer, so Bavarian brewing is far more varied than a single rule suggests.

Beaujolais

A wine region in eastern France, west of the Saône and north of Lyon, south of Burgundy and the Mâconnais. It is mostly red wine from Gamay, divided from regional and Beaujolais-Villages up to ten village crus, with clear differences in soil and style between north and south.

Beijing

The Chinese capital’s wine and baijiu region, with vineyards in Fangshan and Miyun and the light-aroma baijiu producers Niulanshan and Red Star Erguotou in Shunyi; estate wineries on the urban edge and old distilleries make a rare “urban drinks region”.

Belgium

A Western European country whose beer culture was inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2016. Two traditions stand out — Trappist-style abbey ales, centred on monasteries in Wallonia and Flanders, and lambic, a spontaneous-fermentation tradition around Brussels and the Pajottenland.

Bierzo

Red-wine DO in western León, Spain, provisionally recognised on 3 June 1988 and formally confirmed on 11 November 1989. It has 2,300 hectares registered, 981 growers and 69 wineries. Mencía accounts for 70.5% of grape production and the vineyards average about 75 years old. Slate and quartzite slopes and a sheltered basin give wines with ripe red fruit, herbal notes and minerality. Bierzo Alto and Bierzo Bajo are common geographic terms, not legal sub-zones.

Bordeaux

A wine region and port city in the Gironde department of south-west France; the Gironde estuary divides the left and right banks, and an oceanic climate with gravel and clay-limestone soils shapes a style centred on Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.

Burgenland

The easternmost federal-state wine region of Austria, shaped by the hot continental Pannonian climate and covering about 11,538 hectares; known for Blaufränkisch reds, Pinot Blanc and Chardonnay from the Leitha Range, and botrytis sweet wines from the Seewinkel east of Lake Neusiedl; it holds six DACs, Mittelburgenland from 2005, Leithaberg, Eisenberg, Neusiedlersee, Rosalia from 2018 and Ruster Ausbruch from 2020.

Burgundy

A wine region in eastern France, west of the Saône, built on Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. From north to south it runs through Chablis, the Côte d'Or, the Côte Chalonnaise and the Mâconnais, with four tiers — regional, village, Premier Cru and Grand Cru.

Campbeltown

The smallest of Scotland's five protected whisky names, legally the South Kintyre ward of Argyll and Bute; more than thirty distilleries operated in the Victorian era, while today only Springbank, Glen Scotia and Glengyle remain, with sea air, oil and light peat as common stylistic clues.

Canada

The national Canadian whisky category, made from corn, rye and barley, mashed, distilled and aged at least three years in wood; caramel and flavouring (other spirits or wine) are permitted, and the 9.09% figure is an age-claim and export-certificate threshold rather than a compositional cap; the style is usually soft and approachable.

Casablanca Valley

Legal sub-region of Chile's Aconcagua wine region, north-west of Santiago on the road to Valparaíso. It was first planted in 1982 by Concha y Toro winemaker Pablo Morandé and formally defined by Decree 464 in 1994. Pacific cold current and morning fog make it one of Chile's earliest cool-climate regions. Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir lead, with granite clay and frost-free higher sites as the main terroir differences. Origin, variety and vintage claims follow Chilean wine law.

Cava

The Spanish appellation for traditional-method sparkling wine, covering Penedès in Catalonia and several other authorised areas; the main varieties are Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada, often with Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, and bottle ageing on the lees splits into Cava de Guarda and Cava de Guarda Superior at minimums of 9 and 18 months.

Central Otago

New Zealand's southernmost wine region, inland Otago around Queenstown, Cromwell and Alexandra. The 2026 vineyard report records 2,116 hectares planted and 2,045 producing, about 5% of the national total. Pinot Noir is about 80% of the vineyard and the flagship variety. A dry semi-continental climate with large summer-winter and day-night ranges, frost risk and site selection driven by mountains and lakes. Six sub-regions are Gibbston, Bannockburn, Cromwell/Lowburn/Pisa, Bendigo, Wanaka and Alexandra. The Central Otago GI was registered on 29 October 2018, and labels follow the 85% rule.

Chablis

The northernmost white-wine region of Burgundy, in the Serein valley of the Yonne, with about 5800 ha of vines. Kimmeridgian limestone and marl rich in marine fossils, a cool climate and Chardonnay alone define it, across four tiers from Petit Chablis to Grand Cru, the latter with only 7 climats.

Champagne

A sparkling wine appellation in northern France, centred on Reims and Épernay, with vineyards in the Montagne de Reims, the Vallée de la Marne, the Côte des Blancs and the Côte des Bar to the south. It is made from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier by the traditional method of in-bottle second fermentation, aged on the lees and labelled by sweetness level.

Châteauneuf-du-Pape

An appellation in the Southern Rhône north of Avignon, granted AOC status in 1936. Galets roulés on the La Crau plateau, the mistral and Grenache-led blends form its backbone; the permitted list is commonly summarised as 13 varieties, but it is a permitted range rather than a fixed recipe.

Chianti Classico

A red-wine DOCG between Florence and Siena in Tuscany whose zone of about 71,800 hectares covers Castellina, Gaiole, Greve and Radda in Chianti in full plus parts of four other municipalities; a 1716 decree fixed the boundary, 33 producers founded the consortium in Radda on 14 May 1924 and adopted the Black Rooster, and the appellation became an independent DOCG on 5 August 1996; Sangiovese must be at least 80%, Annata 12% alcohol and 12 months, Riserva 12.5% and 24 months, Gran Selezione 30 months.

Chile

A wine-producing country on the west coast of South America, with about 126,249 hectares of vineyards in the 2024 SAG count, of which 116,961 are wine varieties and 9,287 are for Pisco; reds are 72.5%; the origin system comes from Decree 464 of 1994, with four levels from Región Vitícola to Subregión, Zona and Área; Maipo, in the Metropolitan Region, has a Mediterranean climate, Andean alluvial soils and about 5,517 hectares of Cabernet Sauvignon; Casablanca, in Valparaíso, is cooled by Pacific fog, was first planted in 1982 and is known for Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay.

Colchagua

Wine zone within the Rapel sub-region of Chile's Central Valley, covering Colchagua and Cardenal Caro provinces. On Wines of Chile's variety figures the main varieties total about 28,600 hectares, led by Cabernet Sauvignon at 11,928, Carmenère at 3,657 and Merlot at 3,132. A Mediterranean climate, warm and dry inland and cooler near the Pacific in the west. Soils are alluvial terraces, Andean foothill deposits and coastal granitic hills. Legal areas include San Fernando, Chimbarongo, Nancagua and Santa Cruz, and Reserva is not a quality grade.

Columbia Valley

AVA covering central and south-central Washington, south-eastern Washington and north-central Oregon, formally designated on 13 November 1984. It totals about 11 million acres (roughly 4.5 million hectares), with 60,079 acres (24,313 hectares) of vineyard on the Washington side and over 99% of the state's vinifera acreage. Annual rainfall is only 6–8 inches, so irrigation is essential. Loess over gravel and slackwater sediment sits on basalt bedrock. Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Merlot, Riesling and Syrah lead. An AVA label requires at least 85% from the named area.

Constantia

Ward of the Cape Town district in the Western Cape, near False Bay. Simon van der Stel was granted the land in 1685 and named it Constantia; it was divided in 1716 into Groot Constantia, Klein Constantia and Bergvliet. Rainfall is about 1,000 mm and the February mean is 20.6 C, so irrigation is rarely needed. Sauvignon Blanc and other whites are important, and Muscat de Frontignan makes the sweet Vin de Constance. Constantia is a ward, not a district; SAWIS reported the Cape Town statistical area at 2,511 hectares in 2024. Wine of Origin requires 100% origin and 85% for variety and vintage.

Côte de Beaune

The white-wine heart of Burgundy's southern Côte d'Or, running from Ladoix-Serrigny to Santenay, with about 5,000 hectares of vines together with the Hautes-Côtes de Beaune and 25 AOCs, including 16 village appellations, 8 grands crus and around 300 premiers crus; Chardonnay dominates the white wines, Corton is the only grand cru mainly producing red wine, and Beaune is the centre of the négociant trade and the region's auction.

Côte de Nuits

The red-wine heart of Burgundy's northern Côte d'Or, running from south of Dijon to Corgoloin, with about 3,000 hectares of vines together with the Hautes-Côtes de Nuits and 39 AOCs, including 14 village appellations, 24 grands crus and around 120 premiers crus; Pinot Noir accounts for about 95% of plantings, the vineyards follow a Jurassic limestone slope about 40 km long, and the grands crus include Clos de Vougeot.

A sake region in Kyoto City's Fushimi ward, known for the Fushui spring water, the Horikawa canal and Jikkokubune boats, and the Gekkeikan and Kizakura brewery museums; in 1889 a Fushimi brewers' tasting drew 35 exhibitors, making it an early arena for ginjo technique. The “Islands” of Scotch whisky are not a legal region but a grouping of island distilleries outside Islay, including Arran, Jura, Skye, Lewis and Orkney; styles range from light and floral to peated and maritime, so no single flavour defines them.

Islay

A whisky island in the Inner Hebrides, about 600 square kilometres in area with a population of roughly 3200. Phenols from peat-dried malt, sea air and long maturation shape its image, yet the island also makes unpeated and lightly peated whisky, so Islay is not simply a synonym for heavy peat.

Italy

A major wine-producing country in southern Europe, with vineyards in almost all of its 20 regions; known for native grape varieties, the DOCG/DOC/IGT hierarchy and a range of styles from sparkling wine to bitter aperitifs.

Itata Valley

The Valle del Itata sub-region of Chile's Sur region, covering parts of Ñuble and Biobío, with legal areas including Chillán, Quillón, Portezuelo and Coelemu. On Wines of Chile's variety figures the main varieties total about 10,000 hectares, led by País at 3,877, Moscatel de Alejandría at 3,675 and Cinsault at 831. A Mediterranean climate moderated by the Pacific, with wet winters and dry summers, and many old vines are dry-farmed. Soils are granite, volcanic deposits and schist, and the decree also defines the Secano Interior special D.O. Reserva is not a quality grade.

Jamaica

Japan

Jura

A wine region in eastern France between Burgundy and Switzerland, known for limestone and marl slopes and local varieties such as Savagnin, Poulsard and Trousseau; vin jaune and vin de paille are its signatures, and it also has appellations for red, white and sparkling wines.

Kagoshima

The core honkaku shochu region of southern Kyushu, centred on Satsuma sweet-potato shochu made with white, black, or yellow koji through a two-stage mash and pot-still distillation; the prefecture has more than 80 breweries and over 2000 honkaku shochu labels, with atmospheric and vacuum distillation, kame storage, and a 25-degree product convention shaping its flavour and market.

Kentucky

A US state and the dominant production area for bourbon, with about 95% of global bourbon supply; known for corn-based mashbills, new charred oak and limestone water, though bourbon is a nationwide US category and Kentucky is not a legal synonym.

Kyoto

A prefecture-wide name for sake in Kyoto, divided into Rakuchu, Fushimi, Kyoto Tanba, Miyazu, Fukuchiyama, and Kyotango-Mineyama brewing associations; Rakuchu is represented by small breweries such as Sasaki Shuzo, founded in 1893, and Kyoto sake is closely tied to Kyoto cuisine, temple vegetarian food, and pickles.

A wine region band along the Loire river in western France, stretching from Nantes to the Centre-Loire across different climates and soils, with Chenin Blanc, Sauvignon Blanc, Cabernet Franc and Gamay as core varieties and a range of dry whites, sweet wines, reds and sparkling wines.

Lowland

One of the five protected Scotch whisky regions, lying south of a line between Dundee and Greenock; often summarised as grassy, citrusy and creamy, yet home both to old triple-distilling sites and to farm-scale newcomers, so lightness is a starting point rather than a rule.

A city at the meeting of the Yangtze and Tuo rivers in southern Sichuan and a core area of Luzhou Laojiao and strong-aroma baijiu; a place to see old cellars, river transport and urban liquor production together.

The appellation of the Minho region in north-west Portugal, known for fresh, low-alcohol whites shaped by Atlantic influence; in the name, verde refers to youth rather than colour, with a minimum actual alcohol of 8% vol for regular wines, 9% vol when a sub-zone is named and 11.5% vol when Alvarinho is declared, and a maximum total alcohol of 14% vol; the main white varieties are Alvarinho, Loureiro, Arinto, Avesso, Trajadura and Azal, with Vinhão leading the reds.

Vouvray

A district east of Tours in the Loire, made entirely from Chenin Blanc and covering dry, off-dry, sweet and sparkling styles; tuffeau limestone caves, a cool climate and its 1936 AOC make it the best place to understand Chenin’s range.

Wachau

A designated wine region in the Danube gorge of Lower Austria between Melk and Krems, with about 1,340 hectares of steep terraces and dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling from crystalline rock and loess; a Wachau DAC since the 2020 vintage, plus the Steinfeder, Federspiel and Smaragd style marks that the Vinea Wachau association has used since 1983 and defines by finished alcohol at up to 11.5% vol, 11.5–12.5% vol and at least 12.5% vol.

Walker Bay

District in the Cape South Coast region of the Western Cape, around Hermanus. It has three Hemel-en-Aarde wards and Bot River. Cooled by the Benguela Current and sea breezes. Soils are mainly Bokkeveld shale, with granite and Table Mountain sandstone on higher slopes. Pinot Noir and Chardonnay lead, with Sauvignon Blanc, Pinotage and Syrah also planted. Tim Hamilton Russell bought 170 hectares in 1975 and released the first vintage in 1981. SAWIS reported Cape South Coast at 2,671 hectares in 2024 with no district figure. Wine of Origin requires 100% origin and 85% for variety and vintage.

Willamette Valley

Willamette Valley wine region in north-west Oregon, established as an AVA in 1983. The AVA covers about 3,428,800 acres (1,387,600 hectares), with 25,450 acres (10,300 hectares) of vineyard and about two-thirds of Oregon's wineries. The Coast Range and Cascades shelter a mild, long growing season; soils are marine sedimentary, volcanic and loess. Pinot Noir dominates, first planted in 1965, alongside Pinot Gris, Chardonnay and Riesling. There are 11 nested AVAs, and Oregon requires 90% for a variety label against the federal 75%.

Xinjiang

A wine region in north-western China centred on the northern Tianshan foothills, the Ili valley and the Yanqi basin; a temperate continental arid climate brings strong sun and wide day-night swings, and gravelly soils with drip irrigation and winter vine burial define local viticulture, with Cabernet Sauvignon and other international varieties dominant in China's key grape base.

Yamanashi

Japan's core wine region, centred on the Kofu basin and Katsunuma; Koshu is the signature local white variety, and alluvial-fan gravel soils amid the mountains around Mount Fuji give taut, restrained whites with clear acidity, while Katsunuma is also one of the birthplaces of the modern Japanese wine industry.

Yarra Valley

A cool-climate region northeast of Melbourne, Victoria, planted from 1838 and one of Australia’s earliest wine districts; known for Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Shiraz, it received GI status in the 1990s and leans toward Burgundy and the Northern Rhône in style.

Yibin

A city at the confluence of the Jinsha and Min rivers in southern Sichuan and the home of Wuliangye; a useful place to connect strong-aroma baijiu, river geography and distillery visits.

Yunnan

A province in south-west China whose wine regions range from the Shangri-La highlands of Diqing to the low-altitude Mile basin; Deqin vineyards lie between 1800 and 2800 m in the dry valleys of the Jinsha and Mekong rivers, with high plots reaching 2400–2800 m, while Mile vineyards sit at about 1480 m, creating a sharp contrast in sunshine, temperature range, ultraviolet intensity and variety choice.

Zhejiang Province

An eastern coastal province and the core yellow wine region of China; Shaoxing wine combines glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water, with tanfan, linfan and weifan rice-handling routes and jiafan-style products, while Jinhua and Huzhou keep traditions of their own.

Zunyi

A prefecture-level city in northern Guizhou, home to the Chishui River and Maotai Town; a core area of sauce-aroma baijiu where valley climate, qu making and distillery visits can be connected.
