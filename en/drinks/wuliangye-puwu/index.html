"Puwu" is the market nickname for Wuliangye's core product, usually the eighth-generation Wuliangye. It belongs to strong-aroma baijiu, commonly 52% ABV in 500 mL, and comes from Yibin, Sichuan. Understanding this product means looking at the brand, the producer and the specification together, not only at the three characters "Wuliangye".

Figure: Wuliangye Puwu. Photograph: File:Vertreter der vier Baijiu Kategorien.jpg. Credit: Xiaomage de, CC BY-SA 4.0 (source page). Category image; not a claim that it shows the exact product, site or event.

Definition and position

"Puwu" is not an official product name but an abbreviation used by consumers and channels for the core product, distinguishing it from 1618, series products and commemorative editions. It belongs to the strong-aroma route of baijiu and is positioned as the brand's core product rather than an entry-level or limited release.

The same brand also has lower-strength versions and different packaging editions. Strength, volume, packaging and batch all affect the experience and the price, so the two characters "Puwu" do not determine everything about a bottle; the label still governs.

Producer and brand

The product is made within the group system, the brand is carried by Wuliangye, and production and sales sit with the listed platform. Group, listed company, brand and product are four layers with different data scopes and responsible parties.

When you see "Puwu ex-factory price" or "Puwu wholesale price", first establish whether it is the official guide price, a channel transaction price or a market quote in a given year. Price information is highly time-sensitive; this entry gives no figures and only explains the scope difference.

Origin and raw materials

Yibin lies in southern Sichuan, where the Min and Jinsha rivers meet before joining the Yangtze. The humid valley climate suits open qu making and long pit fermentation. Within the map of China's baijiu regions, Yibin is an important strong-aroma node.

The raw materials are five grains: sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat and corn. Sorghum provides the frame and structure, rice and glutinous rice add sweetness, wheat brings qu aroma and fermentation power, and corn adds body. The ratio changes by product line; multi-grain is a style route, not a quality grade.

Brewing process

Strong aroma uses solid-state mud-pit fermentation, continued mash recycling and mixed steaming and boiling. Qu making uses wheat as the main material, and the microbial composition of qu affects pit and grain aroma; the longer a pit is used continuously, the more stable the pit-mud ecology.

After removal, the mash is distilled with fractional cutting: head and tail are separated and the middle run becomes the main base spirit. New spirit is stored by grade and round, then blended to unify style. Blending balances aroma, body and finish rather than simply adding spirits or water.

Specification and label

The common specification is 52% ABV in 500 mL; there are also 39% ABV and other lower-strength versions plus different volumes and gift editions. The product standard for strong-aroma baijiu is commonly GB/T 10781.1, and the bottle label governs.

The producer, product name, aroma type, strength, volume, standard and anti-counterfeit information on the label are the basics for identity. Buy through official channels and check anti-counterfeit marks and batches rather than judging from packaging similarity or price alone.

Flavour and tasting

The aroma is led by pit, grain and sweet notes, with a mellow sweet entry, a relatively full body, an opening mid-palate and a clean finish. At 52% ABV the heat is more noticeable, while 39% ABV is softer; strength affects entry and aroma release but does not establish a quality ranking.

For Wuliangye Puwu, taste from a small cup: smell at rest, then gently swirl and smell again. Let the liquid stay in the mouth and note the order of sweet, sour, bitter and savoury and the body thickness. Water lowers heat and opens aroma but changes balance, so add drop by drop and compare.

History and market

Common public milestones are: the distillery was formally named Yibin Wuliangye Distillery in 1959, the group was restructured in 1998, and the listed arm was listed in the same year. The core product has gone through several generations, and "eighth generation" is the current common description; the exact edition and packaging follow official information.

Puwu has long sat in the high-end price band and is a common choice for banquets and gifting. Prices change with year, channel and supply and demand, and this entry gives no figures. Treating price as proof of quality, or treating "vintage" as age, are common misreadings.

Buying and storage

Prefer official channels and check anti-counterfeit marks, producer and batch.

For Wuliangye Puwu, unopened baijiu should be stored upright, away from light, in a cool place without strong odours. Once opened, drink it soon; prolonged exposure loses aroma. Home storage cannot reproduce distillery conditions, so purchase year should not be equated with quality gain.

Common misconceptions

First, treating "Puwu" as an official name. It is a market nickname. Second, treating series products as Puwu. They are different product lines. Third, assuming higher strength is more authentic. Strength serves product design. Fourth, treating a packaging edition as a body upgrade. Edition differences need official explanation. Fifth, judging authenticity from price or packaging. Anti-counterfeit checks, channels and labels are more reliable.
