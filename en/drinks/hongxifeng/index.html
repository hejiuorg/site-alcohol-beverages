Hong Xifeng is a high-end strategic product of Shaanxi Xifeng and belongs to feng-aroma baijiu, commonly 52% ABV in 500 mL and made in Liulin Town, Fengxiang, Shaanxi. Its signature process combines multi-stage pit fermentation with ageing in wine-sea containers; the "red" in the name refers to product identity, not age.

Figure: Hong Xifeng. Photograph: File:Vertreter der vier Baijiu Kategorien.jpg. Credit: Xiaomage de, CC BY-SA 4.0 (source page). Category image; not a claim that it shows the exact product, site or event.

Definition and position

Hong Xifeng is the product, Xifeng is the brand, Shaanxi Xifeng Distillery Co., Ltd. is the producer, and feng aroma is the category. The four levels cannot be mixed: company history is not the age of the liquid, and brand recognition is not the quality of one specific product.

Within the brand system, Hong Xifeng sits in the high-end and super-high-end bands and carries image and gifting roles. Other series such as Wine-sea Cellar, Colourful Xifeng and the old green bottle sit below or beside it. Product lines may share a tradition, but raw-material ratios, base-spirit grades and storage times differ.

Origin and production

Xifeng is made in Liulin Town, Fengxiang District, Baoji, Shaanxi. The site lies in the western Guanzhong Plain, where groundwater and pit conditions suit feng-aroma production. On the map of China's baijiu regions, Shaanxi sits at the boundary between northern light-aroma and feng-aroma areas; the drier climate than the Sichuan Basin gives fermentation and storage a different micro-ecology.

Origin, company, brand and product should be read separately: origin explains natural conditions, the company explains the producer, the brand explains market identity, and the product explains the specification. The producer address and production date on the pack are the direct way to check a specific bottle.

Ingredients and qu making

Feng-aroma baijiu uses sorghum as its main raw material, with sorghum providing starch and structure; barley, wheat and peas are often used for qu. Qu making uses medium-to-high-temperature qu, and the temperature, humidity and turning schedule in the qu room shape the microbial community and the aroma direction.

Raw materials and qu quality set the fermentation starting point, but exact ratios belong to company process information and public sources usually give only a range. Readers need not chase an exact formula; the label's aroma type, strength, net volume and product standard, plus the company's public process route, matter more.

Brewing process

Xifeng uses solid-state fermentation in underground earthen pits through several production stages, including pit establishment, pit breaking, pit topping, round pit, pit insertion and pit selection. It uses continued-mash mixed steaming and boiling, then cuts the run during distillation. Different pit stages and rounds give different styles, which are combined during blending to form a complete structure.

The public process description for Hong Xifeng emphasises round-pit spirit as the backbone, blended with spirit from the breaking, topping, insertion and selection stages to give a round, full body. The new spirit then ages in wine-sea containers. These large wicker vessels are lined on the inside and allow limited air exchange, so the liquid changes slowly over long storage, developing aged and honeyed notes.

Specification and label

Public product pages show the Hong Xifeng series commonly at 52 degrees in 500 mL, with Five-star Hong Xifeng positioned as a collector edition. Alcohol by volume and volume are specification information and not a quality grade. Feng-aroma baijiu commonly declares GB/T 14867, subject to the bottle label.

The label should be checked for producer, product name, aroma type, strength, net volume, product standard, production date or batch and anti-counterfeit information. Gift boxes, commemorative editions and regular editions differ in packaging, but the label is the identity basis; do not judge from bottle colour or outer box alone.

Flavour and tasting

Feng aroma is typically described as mellow and elegant, sweet and refreshing, harmonious and long in the finish. Hong Xifeng adds wine-sea aged aroma and honeyed notes, with a relatively soft entry, a full body and a sweet, aged aftertaste. At 52% ABV the alcohol heat is noticeable, so small cups and slow sipping suit it.

For tasting, smell the resting aroma first, then gently swirl the cup. Let the liquid cover the tongue and note the order of sweet, sour, bitter and aged notes. Room temperature is fine; ice dilutes the aroma. Shaanxi dishes, noodles, braised food and roasted meat pair well, while heavy spice can mask the finer aromas.

History and naming

Xifeng has a long brewing tradition. The company sums up its cultural lineage as "three thousand years" and treats wine-sea storage as a key identifier. This is corporate narrative and should be read separately from verifiable modern product history; this entry does not treat a cultural timeline as the age of the liquid.

In 2021 the company held an event marking two years since Hong Xifeng's launch and introduced Five-star Hong Xifeng. In 2022 Five-star Hong Xifeng received the China Alcoholic Drinks Association's "Qingzhuo Award" for a new liquor product. The "red" in the name comes from product identity and gifting settings, while "Xifeng" carries brand and origin memory.

Buying and drinking

Start with the base and the stated strength. From there, consider the sweetness or bitterness, then how it is served. That is how a practical judgment about Hong Xifeng is built.

After opening, store away from light in a cool place, upright, and tighten the cap to reduce aroma loss. Drink with enough water and food and avoid drinking quickly on an empty stomach; for gifting, keep the outer box and anti-counterfeit information. Excessive drinking harms health and minors should not drink.

Common misconceptions

First, treating Hong Xifeng as the whole of Xifeng. It is one high-end series within the brand system. Second, treating "three thousand years" as the age of the liquid. It is corporate storytelling. Third, equating feng aroma with strong aroma. Feng aroma has its own standard and process route. Fourth, treating wine-sea ageing as a uniform process for every product. Base spirits and storage differ by product line. Fifth, using price or packaging instead of the label. Producer, batch and anti-counterfeit information are more reliable.

In brief: A high-end strategic product of Shaanxi Xifeng, in the feng-aroma category; commonly 52% ABV in 500 mL and made in Liulin Town, Fengxiang, Shaanxi. Its signature process combines multi-stage pit fermentation with ageing in wine-sea containers.

Baijiu and Qu making answer different questions about Hong Xifeng: style and region and vintage. Read them as a pair, not as alternatives.
