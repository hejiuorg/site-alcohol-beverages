Jiugui Neican

"Jiugui Neican" is a high-end fuyu-aroma product under the Jiugui brand, commonly bottled at 52% ABV in 500 mL and made in Jishou, Xiangxi, Hunan. It sits at the top of the brand's price range, layered above the Jiugui and Xiangquan series; understanding it means separating brand, producer, aroma type and exact specification.

Figure: Jiugui Neican. Photograph: File:Vertreter der vier Baijiu Kategorien.jpg. Credit: Xiaomage de, CC BY-SA 4.0 (source page). Category image; not a claim that it shows the exact product, site or event.

Definition and position

The Neican series is positioned for the high end and for gifting, and it is the core product in Jiugui's move upmarket. It belongs to the fuyu aroma type, not to a single category such as strong, light or sauce aroma; company material summarises this aroma as a process route that combines features of several aroma types.

"Neican" is a series name, not a quality grade, and not proof of scarcity in the sense of an "internal reference". The product name and the brand name are written the same way, so the series name is needed in conversation to point to a specific bottle.

Producer and brand

The product is made by Jiugui Liquor Co., Ltd. and the brand is Jiugui. The company's predecessor was the Jishou distillery founded in 1956, it listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange in 1997, and in 2016 it became a member enterprise of COFCO.

Brand and listed company are two layers: the brand answers "what is it called and who is it for", while the listed company answers "who owns the trademark and who carries legal responsibility". The company also owns the Jiugui and Xiangquan series, so the style of Neican cannot describe every product it makes.

Origin and raw materials

Jishou in Xiangxi lies in the Wuling Mountains, with a humid climate and four distinct seasons; local cave and spring resources are often used in the production-area narrative. Within the map of China's baijiu regions, Xiangxi belongs to an area with features of several aroma types, forming a contrast with the strong and sauce aroma bands of Sichuan and Guizhou.

Public product material summarises the raw materials of fuyu aroma as multi-grain. Sorghum provides the frame and structure, rice adds sweetness, wheat participates in qu making and supplies fermentation power, and water affects the cleanliness of the entry and the harmony of the body. The exact ingredient list and ratio are governed by the bottle label.

For Jiugui Neican, a multi-grain recipe is a style route, not a league table in which more grains means better quality. Treating pure grain as the only standard ignores the effect of qu, pits, fermentation length and storage on the body.

Process

The fuyu-aroma process route stresses the connection between features of several aroma types. Qu making determines the microbial community and aroma precursors, fermentation completes saccharification and alcohol production in the pit, and distillation cuts the run by stage, with the middle section used as the main base spirit.

Company material describes fuyu aroma as a route combining parts of strong, light and sauce aroma, but this is not "mixing three liquors together". Aroma precursors form during qu making and fermentation; distillation and storage merely organise those precursors into the final style.

After storage, the base spirits are blended to unify aroma, body and finish. Blending manages batch variation and style consistency; it is not simple addition or dilution. When judging whether a fuyu aroma is coherent, look at how the aromas connect and how the finish behaves, not only at the category label.

Specification and label

Public product material gives the common specification as 52% ABV in 500 mL, with the aroma type marked as fuyu aroma. Baijiu industry terminology and aroma classification can be checked against GB/T 15109, while the specific product standard and grade are governed by the bottle label.

The producer, product name, aroma type, strength, net volume, raw materials, standard and anti-counterfeit information on the label are the basic identity checks. Buy through regular channels and verify the anti-counterfeit mark and batch rather than judging by ceramic bottle or packaging shape.

Aroma and tasting

The nose is led by pit aroma, grain aroma and aged aroma, with both elegant and full layers. The entry is mellow and sweet, the body is fairly full, the middle opens and the finish is clean. At 52% ABV the aroma releases directly and the alcohol is fairly noticeable; drinkers unused to high-strength baijiu should use a small glass and drink slowly.

For Jiugui Neican, taste in a small glass: smell the resting spirit first, then swirl gently and smell again. Let the liquid stay in the mouth and follow the order of sweet, sour, bitter and umami, along with body weight. Adding water lowers the alcohol and opens the aroma, but it also changes the balance, so add drop by drop and compare.

History and market

The Jishou distillery was founded in 1956 and is the industrial predecessor of the brand. In 1983 Xiangquan liquor switched to an earthen-ochre jar with incised lines, and in 1987 Huang Yongyu designed a sack-shaped bottle and wrote the two characters "Jiugui", forming the brand's early cultural-liquor image. The company listed in 1997 and became a COFCO member enterprise in 2016.

The Neican series later became an important lever in the company's high-end price band. The annual report shows that Neican revenue and its share of the total move with the market, indicating that high-end products depend closely on channel, supply and demand and brand investment. Prices move with vintage, channel and supply and demand, and this entry gives no figures.

Buying and storage

For Jiugui Neican, buy through regular channels and check the anti-counterfeit mark, producer and batch. Gift boxes and channel-specific editions should be checked against the label, so that a packaging difference is not mistaken

For Jiugui Neican, unopened baijiu should be kept upright, away from light, in a cool place without strong odours. Once opened, drink it reasonably soon; prolonged exposure to air costs aroma. A home cellar cannot reproduce distillery storage conditions, so the year of purchase should not be treated as an automatic quality gain.

Common misconceptions

First, treating "Neican" as a quality grade. It is a series name. Second, treating fuyu aroma as a simple blend of three aroma types. It has its own process route. Third, treating 1987 as the founding year of the company. The predecessor goes back to the 1956 Jishou distillery. Fourth, assuming 52% ABV suits everyone. High-strength spirit should be judged against personal tolerance and actual experience. Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Anti-counterfeit marks, channels and labels are more reliable.

Start with the base and the stated strength. From there, consider the sweetness or bitterness, then how it is served. That is how a practical judgment about Jiugui Neican is built.

Put Jiugui's style next to Baijiu's region and vintage; the difference marks the boundary.
