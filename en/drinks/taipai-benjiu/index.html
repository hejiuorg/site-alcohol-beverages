Taipai Benjiu

The core bottling of Taipai's Ben series, a 500 mL gift box at 15% ABV, semi-dry and explicitly made without caramel colour; brewed by hand in Hutong, Keqiao from glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water, and distinct from the same producer's vintage Ben Yuanjiu and ordinary jiafan.

Taipai Benjiu is the central bottling of Taipai's "Ben" series: a 500 mL gift box at 15% ABV, semi-dry, with a product page that states plainly "no caramel colour added". "Benjiu" is a name created by Zhejiang Taipai Shaoxing Wine Co., Ltd.. It is not a grade in the national standard, and it is not a synonym for "raw liquor" or "base spirit". Keeping it separate from the same producer's vintage Ben Yuanjiu and from ordinary jiafan is the key to buying the right bottle.

"Benjiu" is a brand name, not a category

GB/T 17946 divides Shaoxing wine by total sugar into yuanhong, jiafan (huadiao), shanniang and xiangxue. "Benjiu" is not one of them. Taipai explains "ben" through five ideas: ben se (original colour), ben xiang (original aroma), ben wei (original taste), ben di (local origin) and ben ren (the person who makes it). The explanation comes from the company, not from the standard. Benjiu is therefore a huangjiu from the Shaoxing protected area, and its formal class is whatever the sweetness type on the back label says, usually semi-dry.

Hutong, 1994 and an export-first company

The plant stands in Hutong Subdistrict, Keqiao District, in what Taipai calls the source of Jian Lake and a Chinese yellow-rice-wine production base. The company was founded in 1994 with investment from Zhejiang Cereals, Oils and Foodstuffs Import and Export Co. It covers 450 mu, with 13 brewing workshops, two bottling workshops and more than 800 employees. Its own profile adds four checkable claims: a 68-year export brand, a site 500 metres from the Jian Lake source, three national-level masters, and the description "China's largest fully handmade yellow-rice-wine producer". The last is a company claim, not an independent ranking.

The short ingredient list

The standard's definition is deliberately narrow: high-quality glutinous rice, wheat and water from Jian Lake inside the protected zone. Rice supplies starch; wheat is made into wheat qu, which provides enzymes and aroma; a starter culture begins fermentation. Starch is first saccharified into fermentable sugar, then converted by fermentation into alcohol and flavour compounds. The shorter the ingredient list, the more the result depends on the qu and on fermentation control. Benjiu's "no caramel" promise makes that dependence even more visible.

Colour without caramel

GB/T 17946 permits caramel colour in Shaoxing wine under GB 2760, while forbidding other substances not produced by fermentation. Benjiu goes further and adds none, so its colour comes entirely from rice, wheat qu and ageing. The product specification is concrete: 500 mL, 15% alcohol by volume, semi-dry, with sugar inside the semi-dry band; the standard sets jiafan (huadiao) total sugar at 15.1–40.0 g/L. Because nothing is adjusted for colour, the wine darkens naturally with age, a point Taipai made at its 2020 Benjiu festival.

Thirty-three steps and a winter start

The Suning listing reduces Benjiu to three claims: no caramel colour, 33 production steps and a start at Lidong, the beginning of winter. The 33-step count is the company's own; a neighbour in the same city describes its premium line as 228 steps. The two numbers use different methods and cannot be compared directly. The Lidong start, by contrast, is a shared regional practice: cold air suppresses spoilage organisms, fermentation begins slowly, and workers control temperature by stirring the vat. Benjiu is a finished-product line with no vintage ladder; the same producer's winter-brew series is the one split into three-, five-, six-, eight- and ten-year tiers.

Benjiu, Ben Yuanjiu and ordinary jiafan

The three names are often confused. Benjiu is a finished wine, commonly in 500 mL and 1 L formats. Ben Yuanjiu is a single-vintage base wine; the company's site has listed 2014 and 2018, in a 6 L jar, a 1.38 L bottle and a 500 mL six-pack. Jiafan is a category name for semi-dry Shaoxing wine made with a higher proportion of rice. The 2014 Ben Yuanjiu was released at the first China Taipai Benjiu Festival in 2020, which shows that the vintage refers to the base wine's production year, not the bottling year.

Four differences from a standard jiafan

First, naming level: Benjiu is a product name, jiafan is a category. Second, caramel colour: Benjiu adds none, while ordinary jiafan may add it within the standard. Third, price: a channel survey puts Benjiu, Benmei and Jiangnanyuan at RMB 298–688, against RMB 15–60 for Qingchun and huadiao jiafan. Fourth, vintage: Ben Yuanjiu carries a single year, while most jiafan carries an age figure or no vintage at all. The same survey notes that Taipai has almost nothing between RMB 40 and RMB 298, a gap that shapes how the brand is sold.

Where the freshness comes from

Benjiu's keyword is freshness. With no caramel colour to add bitterness or a visual cue of age, and with a relatively short ageing period, rice aroma, wheat-qu aroma and fermentation notes sit at the front; 15% ABV and semi-dry acidity give it a clean frame. China Daily quotes the company describing the wine as "fresh, layered and complex". The contrast with Kuaijishan Lanting is direct: Lanting builds aged aroma and weight through long jar ageing, while Benjiu builds clarity and freshness through a younger body.

Reading the vintages 2014 and 2018

The "2014" and "2018" on Ben Yuanjiu are production years of the base wine, not "2014 years of ageing". The 2014 wine was released in 2020 after several years in jar. GB/T 17946 defines wine age as the time a base wine spends in jar, vat, tank or pool after fermentation, and requires the declared age of a blend to be a weighted average. If Ben Yuanjiu is kept as a single lot, its year reads close to a single-vintage base wine; whether every bottle is from one lot and whether any blending occurs should be checked against the producer's specification, which is not fully public.

The price gap: RMB 298–688 and above RMB 1,280

A September 2026 channel survey gives a fairly clear ladder: Taipai Qingchun and huadiao jiafan at RMB 15–60; Jiangnanyuan, Benjiu and Benmei at RMB 298–688; Ben Yuanjiu and vintage wines at RMB 1280–1880; and almost nothing between RMB 40 and RMB 298. That structure explains why Benjiu acts as Taipai's premium shopfront: it has to catch drinkers trading up, yet the brand offers few steps between everyday bottles and this one. Prices move with channel, region and promotion, and are not a quality scale.

At the table: steamed, blanched and raw-cured

A fresh semi-dry wine suits clean, ingredient-led cooking: steamed fish, blanched prawns, white-cut chicken, drunken shrimp and raw-cured seafood. At 15% ABV it can handle fishiness, and its acidity lifts flavour without the heavier aged notes that would cover the food. With red-braised pork, braised duck or hot pot, Benjiu feels thin; that is the moment for Taipai Handmade Winter Brew, which is rounder and sweeter. Serve Benjiu at room temperature or lightly warmed, never boiled.

The "benjiu is raw liquor" mistake

The most common misreading is to treat Benjiu as "raw liquor" or "original mash". In Taipai's system, Benjiu is a finished bottled line whose selling point is colour without caramel; "raw liquor" and "original wine" correspond to Ben Yuanjiu, which carries a single vintage and is often sold in jars or large formats. The two are different products. The company also uses the phrase "handmade raw mash" for Benjiu, but "raw mash" is not a standard term; whether Benjiu is blended, and in what proportion, is not public, so it cannot be described as "never blended". Nor is Benjiu a "base wine" or an "ingredient wine": it is a finished retail product with a label, an ABV, a net content and an execution standard.

Buying and keeping

Start with the product name on the label: Benjiu, Ben Yuanjiu or Benmei, then check ABV, net content, sweetness type and producer. Gift boxes and standard packs differ mainly in packaging and channel. The product page states a five-year shelf life, but the wine should still be stored upright, away from light and heat. Once opened, refrigerate and finish it soon. Shaoxing wine is one branch of Chinese huangjiu, and Benjiu is one line from one producer; a single bottle does not define a whole region.
