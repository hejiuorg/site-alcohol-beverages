Crystal Jiannanchun is the core strong-aroma product of Jiannanchun, commonly shortened to "Crystal Sword" in the market. It is usually 52% ABV in 500 mL and comes from Mianzhu, Sichuan. Different strengths, volumes and gift editions exist under the same name, so the bottle label governs.

Definition and position

"Crystal Sword" is a market and channel abbreviation; the formal name is usually written as Crystal Jiannanchun. It belongs to the strong-aroma route of baijiu and is the brand's core product in the high-end mainstream price band, not a vintage label or a grade.

Four levels need to be kept apart: Jiannanchun is the brand, Jiannanchun Group is the producer, Crystal Sword is the specific product, and strong aroma is the style category. Treating a product name as a brand name, or a brand history as the age of the liquid, leads to misreading.

Origin and production

Jiannanchun comes from Mianzhu in Sichuan, at the northern edge of the Chengdu Plain and the southern foot of the Longmen Mountains. Rainfall is relatively generous, rivers and groundwater provide a stable water source, and the warm, humid climate affects qu making, fermentation and storage. Within the map of China's baijiu regions, Mianzhu is an important node for Sichuan strong-aroma production.

The company website describes a "multi-grain strong-aroma" route and links the site to a Tang-dynasty court wine and a long brewing tradition. Origin, company and product are three levels: origin explains natural and technical conditions, the company explains the production and business entity, and the product explains the specific specification.

Ingredients and qu making

Multi-grain strong-aroma fermentation commonly uses sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat and corn. Sorghum provides the frame and fermentation base, rice and glutinous rice add sweetness, wheat supports aroma generation, and corn adds body. Exact ratios for particular product lines are not public; the label and product standard are the basis for checking.

Qu making is the starting point of strong-aroma production. Daqu is made from wheat and other grains through grinding, water addition, shaping and incubation in a qu room; temperature and humidity shape the microbial community and thus pit and qu aroma. Qu quality is not judged by one number but by aroma, cross-section and fermentation performance together.

Brewing process

Strong-aroma baijiu uses solid-state fermentation in mud pits, continued mash recycling and mixed steaming and boiling. Pits are used continuously, and the microbial ecology in the pit mud accumulates with years of use, forming pit aroma. Continued mash carries part of the previous round's fermented grain into the next round, keeping flavour and fermentation continuous. Grain, mash and rice husk are proportioned, loaded into the steamer and distilled.

During distillation the run is cut by strength and aroma, with heads, hearts and tails handled separately; the hearts usually form the base spirit. New spirit is stored by grade and round, aged in ceramic jars, then blended. Blending combines base spirits of different rounds and grades to stabilise style; it is not simply adding water to lower strength.

Specification and label

The most common public retail specification is 52% ABV in 500 mL; lower-strength versions, other volumes and gift packs also exist. Alcohol by volume is one label item and cannot stand alone as a quality judgment. Strong-aroma baijiu commonly declares GB/T 10781.1, subject to the bottle label.

The label should be checked for producer name and address, product name, aroma type, strength, net volume, product standard, production date or batch and anti-counterfeit information. Similar packaging does not mean the same batch; buy through official channels and check the anti-counterfeit code and batch.

Flavour and tasting

Typical notes are pit aroma, grain aroma and qu aroma, with a sweet entry, a relatively full body, a clean finish and a sweet aftertaste. At 52% ABV the alcohol heat is more noticeable and aroma release is faster, so small cups and slow sipping suit it; lower-strength versions enter more softly, with different aroma layers and storage stability.

For tasting, smell at rest, then gently swirl the cup and smell again. Let the liquid stay on the tongue and note the order of sweet, sour, bitter and savoury and the body thickness. Room temperature is fine and ice is unnecessary. Sichuan dishes, braised food and red-braised dishes pair well, while heavy spice can mask aroma detail.

History and naming

Mianzhu has a long brewing tradition. The company website links "Jiannan Shao Chun" to a Tang-dynasty court wine and sums up its cultural inheritance as "1500 years". This is corporate narrative and should be read separately from verifiable modern plant history and product development.

"Jiannan" comes from the Tang-dynasty place name Jiannan Circuit, while "chun" often means wine in traditional names. "Crystal Sword" comes from the market description of the transparent bottle and its crystal-like appearance. A name carries brand memory; it is not age, vintage or quality grade, and the label still takes priority.

Buying and drinking

When buying, confirm strength and volume first, then check production date, batch and channel. The regular 52% ABV bottle is common for banquets and gifting, while lower-strength versions may suit everyday drinking; prices vary with year, channel and supply, so no figure is given here.

After opening, store away from light in a cool place and tighten the cap to reduce aroma loss; for long storage keep the bottle upright and avoid high heat. Drink from a small cup, slowly, with enough water and food, and avoid drinking quickly on an empty stomach. Excessive drinking harms health and minors should not drink.

Common misconceptions

First, treating Crystal Sword as a separate brand. It is a product abbreviation of Jiannanchun. Second, treating "1500 years" as the age of the liquid. It is corporate storytelling, not a vintage statement. Third, assuming higher strength means better quality. Strength is a specification; style and batch matter too. Fourth, treating "old pit" as the age of every bottle. Pit age, base-spirit age and the product claim are different concepts. Fifth, using packaging or price instead of the label. Channel and anti-counterfeit information are more direct.
