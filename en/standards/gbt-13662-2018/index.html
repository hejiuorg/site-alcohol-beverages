GB/T 13662-2018 Huangjiu

The current Chinese national standard for Huangjiu (yellow wine), published on 2018-09-17 and effective from 2019-04-01; it fully replaces GB/T 13662-2008.

Terminology, classification, quality requirements and inspection rules for Huangjiu

GB/T 13662-2018 is the current Chinese national standard for Huangjiu, or yellow wine. It sets out terminology and definitions, product classification, requirements, test methods and inspection rules, together with marking, packaging, transport and storage. It answers the question "what grain-based fermented beverage may be called Huangjiu, into which types is it divided, and what basic requirements must it meet"; it does not judge the flavour quality of an individual Huangjiu.

The standard has been revised several times. After the 2008 edition, the 2018 edition adjusted classification, indicators and test methods, and kept "Huangjiu" as the standard title. Unlike baijiu standards, which are split into aroma-type parts, the Huangjiu standard takes the drink type as its object and handles products from different regions and with different sugar levels in one text. Cite the full code "GB/T 13662-2018" to avoid confusion with older editions.

What the standard covers

The standard first defines the raw-material and process boundary of Huangjiu: a beverage made mainly from rice, millet and other grains, through steaming, adding qu or wine starter, fermentation, pressing, filtration, pasteurisation of the wine, storage and blending. Producing regions differ in rice soaking, steaming, cooling, fermentation vessels and post-treatment, but products within this process framework fall within the scope of Huangjiu.

The standard then divides products by sugar level or total sugar and related dimensions, sets requirements for sensory and physicochemical indicators, and specifies corresponding test methods and judgement rules. Labelling, packaging, transport and storage are also within scope, because these stages affect product stability and the consistency of labelling. The standard is a recommended national standard, not a food-safety national standard; contaminants, additives and general labelling rules remain governed by the relevant food-safety standards and regulations.

For readers, the terminology and classification sections are the most useful. They help distinguish Huangjiu from adjacent concepts such as rice wine, sake and cooking wine, and help interpret terms such as "dry", "semi-dry", "semi-sweet" and "sweet" on labels. Class names describe sugar level and style, not quality grade.

Classification and key concepts

The core raw material of Huangjiu is rice. Glutinous rice has a high proportion of amylopectin and becomes highly sticky after steaming, which helps stabilise the mash structure and gives a fuller body. Rice or japonica rice is often used together with glutinous rice, affecting yield, sweetness and thickness. Water sources, rice varieties and ratios in different regions are important sources of stylistic difference.

Qu is central to saccharification and fermentation in Huangjiu. Traditional Huangjiu uses wheat qu, wine starter or a combination of both; wheat qu is made from wheat, while wine starter often contains Rhizopus, yeast and other microorganisms. Control of qu making and fermentation determines saccharification speed, the temperature curve of fermentation and the final flavour. As with daqu and xiaoqu in baijiu, the choice of qu in Huangjiu maps directly onto regional traditions and product types.

By sugar level, Huangjiu is commonly divided into dry, semi-dry, semi-sweet and sweet types. Sugar level comes from the degree of fermentation, raw-material ratios and post-treatment, not from simply adding sugar. Dry Huangjiu has low residual sugar and a relatively fresh taste; sweet Huangjiu retains more sugar and has a thicker body. Consumers can read these names as style hints, but should not equate "sweet" with "low alcohol" or "low quality".

Shaoxing is one of the best-known producing regions for Huangjiu, and Guyuelongshan Huadiao serves as a concrete example of traditional Shaoxing Huangjiu. Shaoxing wine also has a geographical-indication product standard that imposes additional requirements on the area of origin, raw materials and traditional process. Products following that geographical-indication standard and products following only the general Huangjiu standard are subject to different levels of constraint.

How it relates to other standards

Within the standards system, GB/T 13662-2018 is a product standard responsible for quality requirements and inspection rules for Huangjiu. GB/T 17204-2021 Terminology and classification of alcoholic beverages is the horizontal classification standard, which places Huangjiu in the fermented-beverage category and provides the terminology framework at beverage level. Used together, they prevent Huangjiu from being confused with rice wine or sake.

Huangjiu also involves geographical-indication product standards, food-safety national standards and general labelling standards. Geographical-indication standards constrain products from a specific region, food-safety standards constrain contaminants and additives, and labelling standards constrain how information is presented. A product standard cannot replace these documents, and they cannot replace its sensory and physicochemical requirements.

Huangjiu is a drink-type name; Shaoxing wine is a geographical-indication product type within it; Huadiao, Jiafan and Yuanhong are product designations. Mixing drink type, geographical indication and product designation at the same level is one of the most common classification errors in Huangjiu content.

What it means for readers

First, it gives the word "Huangjiu" on a label a checkable basis. Producers can organise production and testing against the standard, and consumers can use it to judge whether a product falls within the process and classification framework of Huangjiu. Whether a label claim is true still needs production records, test reports and regulatory information.

Second, it helps readers understand sugar-level classification. Dry, semi-dry, semi-sweet and sweet describe sugar content and taste tendency, not price or quality grade. When choosing, one can first select a type by drinking context and taste preference, then look at region, brand and age.

Third, it reminds readers to distinguish the general standard from the geographical-indication standard. Following GB/T 13662-2018 means a product meets the general requirements for Huangjiu; following the Shaoxing wine geographical-indication standard additionally means the area of origin, raw materials and process are subject to more specific constraints. The two can coexist but cannot replace each other.

Points to watch

For GB/T 13662-2018 Huangjiu, the standard text is protected by copyright. This standard records only public metadata such as the standard code, title, dates and technical committee, and explains the standard's role and structure in original words. It does not reproduce the standard. For specific clauses, indicators or test steps, consult the official full-text system or a formally published copy.

Cite the full code and year. GB/T 13662 has 2008 and 2018 editions; omitting the year merges different texts. For English expression, "Huangjiu" is the transliteration used in the standard's English title; everyday English also uses yellow wine or Chinese rice wine, but these terms may overlap with other drink types, so formal citation should follow the official English title.

Finally, do not treat a product standard as a regional certification or a brand review. A standard applies to a class of products, not to one region or brand; products at different price points and in different styles can sit under the same standard. Judging a specific drink still requires the label, region, raw materials, process and actual tasting.
