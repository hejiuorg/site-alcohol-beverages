GB 2757-2012 National food safety standard — Distilled spirits and compounded spirits

A mandatory national food safety standard published on 2012-08-06 and effective from 2013-02-01, replacing GB 2757-1981; it covers distilled spirits and compounded spirits and limits methanol to 0.6 g/L for grain-based products and 2.0 g/L for other raw materials, and cyanide as HCN to 8.0 mg/L, both calculated on a 100% alcohol basis. It also requires alcohol content and the warning about excessive drinking on labels. Lead is not set here; it falls under GB 2762.

GB 2757-2012 is the current mandatory food safety standard for distilled spirits and compounded spirits. It was published on 2012-08-06, took effect on 2013-02-01 and replaces GB 2757-1981 and its first and second amendments. It sets a safety floor, not aroma, grade or style: meeting the methanol and cyanide limits does not show high quality. The GB prefix has no GB/T counterpart; a producer cannot choose whether to comply. Any distilled spirit or compounded spirit made or sold in China is subject to it.

Standard number and administration

The number is GB 2757-2012, a mandatory national food safety standard. It was issued by the Ministry of Health of the People's Republic of China; food safety standards are organised and interpreted by the National Health Commission, the successor to the Ministry of Health. They are not assigned to a professional standardisation technical committee and have no recommended version. The foreword notes the changed name and cyanide limit, removed manganese limit and added labelling requirements. Clauses 4.2 to 4.4 took effect on 2013-08-01, and the remaining clauses on 2013-02-01. Copyright in the text belongs to the issuing body; this standard gives a factual description only.

Version history and current status

GB 2757-1981 was a hygiene standard, titled Hygienic standard for distilled spirits and compounded spirits, and was amended twice. The 2012 edition converted it into a food safety standard, renamed it, and rewrote it around raw materials, sensory requirements, physicochemical limits, contaminants and mycotoxins, food additives, and labelling. Manganese was removed because it was no longer a primary risk indicator for these products. Labelling became a mandatory clause, covering alcohol content and the health warning. The standard remains current.

Scope and exclusions

The standard applies to distilled spirits and compounded spirits. A distilled spirit is a beverage alcohol made from grain, tubers, fruit, milk or other main raw materials by fermentation, distillation and blending. A compounded spirit is made with a distilled spirit and/or edible alcohol as the base, with edible auxiliary materials or food additives, by blending, mixing or further processing, so that the original base style is changed. The standard does not apply to fermented alcohol, which is governed by GB 2758, nor to ordinary food, health food or medicines. It does not set the aroma type, grade, total acid or total esters of baijiu, which belong to product standards. Not every lujiu, medicinal liquor or infusion falls under this standard; the base and process determine coverage.

Key indices and limits

There are only two physicochemical limits. Methanol is limited to 0.6 g/L for distilled spirits and compounded spirits made from grain, and to 2.0 g/L for those made from other raw materials such as tubers, fruit or milk. Cyanide, expressed as HCN, is limited to 8.0 mg/L. Both are calculated on a 100% alcohol basis, and the test method is GB/T 5009.48. Conversion recalculates the sample to a pure-alcohol basis using its measured strength before comparison, so a stronger spirit has a larger absolute allowance per litre as sold, but the converted value must still fall within the limit.

Contaminants must comply with GB 2762, mycotoxins with GB 2761, and food additives with GB 2760. It is important to state that GB 2757-2012 does not set a lead limit. Lead is a contaminant and is managed under GB 2762. Plasticisers, pesticide residues and similar hazards are also outside Table 1 and are governed by other food safety standards or regulatory notices.

Test methods

Methanol and cyanide follow the referenced GB/T 5009.48. That method has been revised, so testing should use the edition in force. Contaminants follow the methods corresponding to GB 2762, mycotoxins those corresponding to GB 2761, and additives those corresponding to GB 2760. Because both methanol and cyanide are calculated on a 100% alcohol basis, the test report must also state the measured alcohol content; otherwise the result cannot be recalculated.

Relationship with neighbouring standards

GB 2758 covers fermented alcohol and its compounded products, while this standard covers distilled spirits, so the two are separated by process and cannot be interchanged. GB 2762 controls contaminants including lead, while this standard controls only methanol and cyanide and refers to GB 2762 for the rest. GB 2760 controls additives and is referenced, not reproduced. Product standards such as the GB/T 10781 series set aroma, sensory, total acid, total esters and grade, while this standard sets the safety floor; compliance with one does not imply compliance with the other. GB/T 17204-2021 provides terminology and classification.

Compliance and labelling

Apart from alcohol content, the warning and shelf life, labels must comply with GB 7718. Alcohol content is expressed in %vol. The label must state that excessive drinking is harmful to health, and other warnings may be added. Beverage alcohol at 10%vol or above may be exempt from a shelf-life declaration. These are mandatory requirements, not suggestions. Imported distilled spirits and compounded spirits must also comply, and the Chinese label must carry the alcohol content and warning. A producer cannot omit the warning on grounds of traditional production, nor relabel a compounded spirit to avoid the stricter category; health claims are restricted by food safety and advertising law.

Common misconceptions

First, GB 2757 is not a baijiu product standard; it sets no aroma, grade, total acid or total esters. Second, the standard contains no lead limit; lead is in GB 2762. Third, grain-based production is not a safety guarantee; the grain-based methanol limit is stricter at 0.6 g/L, but process control still matters. Fourth, 0.6 g/L and 2.0 g/L cannot be compared directly with an as-sold concentration; both are calculated on a 100% alcohol basis. Fifth, compounded spirit is not the same as medicinal or health liquor; the definition focuses on a distilled-spirit or edible-alcohol base, added materials and a changed base style. Sixth, meeting this standard is only a safety floor; product, labelling and licensing requirements still apply.
