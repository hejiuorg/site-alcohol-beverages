GB/T 23547-2009 Strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu

Published on 2009-04-14 and effective from 2009-12-01, this standard defines strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu by a harmonious combination of strong and sauce character, classifies it by alcohol content into high-strength and low-strength liquor with premium and first grades, and lists Hubei Baiyunbian and Anhui Kouzijiao among representative products. It was fully replaced by GB/T 10781.8-2021 with effect from 2022-06-01.

GB/T 23547-2009 is the first national standard for strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu. It was published on 2009-04-14 and took effect on 2009-12-01. It defines the style as a harmonious combination of strong and sauce character, classifies products by alcohol content, and sets separate sensory and physicochemical requirements. Baiyunbian in Hubei and Kouzijiao in Anhui are representative products. The standard was fully replaced by GB/T 10781.8-2021 with effect from 2022-06-01, so current work should cite the new standard.

Standard number and administration

The number is GB/T 23547-2009. The GB/T prefix marks a recommended national standard, not a mandatory one, and it is not a food safety standard. It was issued by the General Administration of Quality Supervision, Inspection and Quarantine and the Standardization Administration of China, and administered by the National Technical Committee for Baijiu Standardization (SAC/TC 358). It was built on the industry standard QB/T 2524-2001, which ceased to apply when this standard took effect.

Version history and current status

Mixed aroma was first regulated by QB/T 2524-2001. The 2009 national standard established a full framework of definition, classification, sensory, physicochemical, hygiene, analysis, inspection and labelling. GB/T 10781.8-2021 was published on 2021-11-26 and took effect on 2022-06-01, fully replacing it. The new standard renames the product as Part 8 of the baijiu quality requirements, deletes product classification, keeps premium and first grades, replaces n-propanol with total acid-plus-ester and hexanoic acid plus ethyl hexanoate, and sets separate limits for the first year and later storage.

Scope and exclusions

The standard applies to the production, inspection and sale of strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu. It covers baijiu made from grain by traditional solid-state fermentation, distillation, ageing and blending, without added edible alcohol or non-fermentation aroma and taste substances, and with a distinctive strong-sauce mixed style. It does not cover single strong-aroma or single sauce-aroma baijiu, nor liquid-fermentation baijiu, solid-liquid baijiu or compounded liquor made from edible alcohol and flavourings. It does not establish origin, vintage, price or brand rank. Hygiene was referenced to GB 2757; current compliance also involves GB 2762 and GB 2760.

Key indices and limits

The definition has three elements: grain raw materials; traditional solid-state fermentation, distillation, ageing and blending; and no added edible alcohol or non-fermentation aroma and taste substances. The style is a combination of strong and sauce character, not a simple blend: the ethyl hexanoate system of strong aroma and the high-temperature qu and stacking system of sauce aroma must be harmonised in one body.

Products are classified by alcohol content into high-strength liquor at 41%vol to 68%vol and low-strength liquor at 18%vol to 40%vol. Physicochemical requirements are given in separate tables for the two classes, each with premium and first grades, covering alcohol, total acid, total esters, ethyl hexanoate, n-propanol and solids. A verified example is total acid in high-strength liquor: at least 0.50 g/L for premium and 0.30 g/L for first grade. The remaining limits are set out in Tables 3 and 4 of the standard text and should be read there rather than inferred. The permitted difference between measured and labelled alcohol is ±1.0%vol.

The 2009 limits differ from the 2021 successor. For reference, GB/T 10781.8-2021 sets alcohol at 25.0%vol to 68.0%vol; solids no more than 0.60 g/L; within one year, total acid at least 0.60 g/L for premium and 0.40 g/L for first grade, total esters at least 1.60 and 1.00 g/L, and ethyl hexanoate 0.60 to 2.00 g/L for premium and 0.60 to 1.80 g/L for first grade; after one year, total acid plus esters at least 35.0 and 30.0 mmol/L, and hexanoic acid plus ethyl hexanoate at least 1.20 and 0.80 g/L, all on a 45.0%vol basis.

Test methods

Sensory and physicochemical requirements follow GB/T 10345, Baijiu analysis methods; alcohol later moved to GB 5009.225. Net content follows JJF 1070. Hygiene follows GB 2757. Inspection rules and labelling, packaging, transport and storage follow GB/T 10346, and labels follow GB 10344. The standard has no separate instrumental method for mixed aroma; style remains a sensory judgement. Physicochemical results show only that acids, esters, alcohols and solids fall within the limits, not that the aroma type is correct.

Relationship with neighbouring standards

Compared with GB/T 10781.8-2021, the 2009 edition has product classification, an n-propanol index, no total acid-plus-ester or hexanoic acid plus ethyl hexanoate, and some limits tiered by alcohol content; the new standard deletes classification and n-propanol, adds the two acid-ester indices, and fixes conversion at 45.0%vol. Compared with strong-aroma standards, it requires the high-temperature qu and stacking character of sauce aroma as well as strong-aroma sweetness. Compared with sauce-aroma standards, it does not require high-temperature qu or multi-round collection as a condition. GB/T 15109-2021 defines terms, GB/T 17204-2021 assigns categories, and GB 2757-2012 is the safety floor.

Compliance and labelling

Labels should state the product name, aroma type, alcohol content, net content, production date, producer and standard number. Alcohol is expressed in %vol, with a tolerance of ±1.0%vol. The 2009 edition allows the name strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu and grade marks of premium or first grade, but a producer must match the edition actually declared and cannot cite old limits while declaring the new standard. Products made after 2022-06-01 should use GB/T 10781.8-2021. Food safety requirements remain those of the standards in force.

Common misconceptions

First, GB/T 23547-2009 is no longer current; the current standard is GB/T 10781.8-2021. Second, mixed aroma is not a blend of finished strong-aroma and sauce-aroma spirits; the standard requires one solid-state fermentation system. Third, the 41%vol to 68%vol range is a classification boundary, not a quality ranking. Fourth, premium grade is a level within the standard, not a cross-brand score. Fifth, physicochemical indices alone cannot determine aroma type; sensory style remains primary. Sixth, n-propanol from the old standard should not be carried over to the new one.
