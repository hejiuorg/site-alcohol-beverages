GB/T 26760-2011 Jiang-flavour Chinese spirits

The 2011 Chinese national standard for Jiang-flavour (sauce-aroma) baijiu, published on 2011-07-20 and effective from 2011-12-01; it was withdrawn on 2025-06-01 and fully replaced by GB/T 10781.4-2024.

GB/T 26760-2011 is the Chinese national standard for Jiang-flavour baijiu published in 2011. One point must come first: the standard was withdrawn on 2025-06-01 and fully replaced by GB/T 10781.4-2024 Quality requirements for baijiu — Part 4: Jiangxiangxing baijiu. This standard therefore introduces it as a historical text and terminology source, not as a currently valid product standard. Anyone writing about, testing or citing Jiang-flavour baijiu today should consult the current edition first.

From 2011 to 2025, this standard served for more than a decade as the main basis for product testing and label expression for Jiang-flavour baijiu. The edition published in 2024 did not keep a standalone code for Jiang-flavour baijiu; instead it was folded into the "Quality requirements for baijiu" series as Part 4. This mirrors the treatment of the Nongxiangxing standard and shows that baijiu product standards are being reorganised into aroma-type parts under a common series.

Its place in standards history

Before 2011, quality requirements for Jiang-flavour baijiu were scattered across industry standards, local standards and enterprise standards, and their wording was not fully consistent. The publication of GB/T 26760-2011 gave "Jiang-flavour baijiu" a nationwide product-standard framework, so that production, testing, labelling and trade had a common basis. Its object was Jiang-flavour baijiu made from sorghum, wheat, water and other raw materials through traditional solid-state fermentation, distillation, storage and blending.

The title uses "Jiang-flavour baijiu" rather than "quality requirements for Jiang-flavour baijiu", a common style for product standards of that generation. The 2024 edition is titled "Quality requirements for baijiu — Part 4: Jiangxiangxing baijiu", placing the aroma-type name inside the series title. The difference reflects a change in drafting approach and reminds readers that an old standard code belongs to a text system that has since been updated.

Scope and key concepts

The process features of Jiang-flavour baijiu are commonly summarised as high-temperature qu making, two rounds of feeding, multiple rounds of steaming, fermentation and spirit collection, long storage and blending. It uses sorghum as the main raw material and wheat to make high-temperature qu, completing multiple fermentation rounds in an open environment. The thermotolerant microbes and enzymes in high-temperature qu are an important source of the sauce-like, roasted and baked notes.

In style terms, Jiang-flavour baijiu is often described as having a prominent sauce-like aroma, elegance and finesse, a mellow body and a long finish. These are sensory descriptions, not chemical indicators, and they do not constitute a brand ranking. The Chishui River basin in Guizhou is the most concentrated producing region for Jiang-flavour baijiu, and Moutai is one of its most representative products. Jiang-flavour baijiu is not, however, produced only in Guizhou; Sichuan, Hunan and other places also make Jiang-flavour products, and differences in raw materials, climate and process produce different styles.

"Jiang-flavour" in the standard is both an aroma-type name and a product-category boundary. It groups baijiu with a shared process route and shared sensory features for testing and communication; it does not mean that every Jiang-flavour baijiu reaches the same quality level, nor that other aroma types cannot produce similar flavours.

Relationship with current standards

The current replacement is GB/T 10781.4-2024. It was published on 2024-05-28, became effective on 2025-06-01, and fully replaces GB/T 26760-2011. The relationship between old and new is not a simple renaming: Jiang-flavour baijiu is brought into the "Quality requirements for baijiu" series, sharing the same drafting logic with parts such as Nongxiangxing baijiu. Reading the new edition alongside this standard helps clarify the old edition's position and boundaries.

At a higher level, GB/T 15109-2021 Terminology of baijiu industry provides definitions for baijiu-industry terms, and GB/T 17204-2021 Terminology and classification of alcoholic beverages provides the classification framework at beverage level. Product standards set specific quality requirements, terminology standards set conceptual boundaries, and classification standards set broad category placement. Used together, they clarify "what this drink is, which standard tests it, and how it is expressed on a label".

Baijiu is the broader category, and Jiang-flavour is one aroma-type branch. Equating Jiang-flavour with baijiu, or equating one Jiang-flavour brand with the whole aroma type, are common category confusions.

What it means for readers

First, seeing GB/T 26760-2011 on an old label should not lead one to conclude that the product is "outdated" or "non-compliant". Withdrawal means that new production and testing should move to the current standard, but products already produced and lawfully circulating may still carry the old code; compliance judgement depends on the production date, label statements and regulatory requirements.

Second, the standard helps readers understand that "Jiang-flavour" is not a marketing word but a type name with a process and testing basis. It asks raw materials, process and sensory features to meet a certain consensus, but it does not decide flavour preference for the consumer.

Third, standard replacement reminds readers to watch editions. GB/T 26760-2011 and GB/T 10781.4-2024 differ in code, title and some technical requirements. When writing, translating or comparing products, state which edition is being followed and avoid mixing the two.

Points to watch

The standard text is protected by copyright. This standard records only public metadata such as the standard code, title, dates, technical committee and replacement relationship, and explains the standard's historical role in original words. It does not reproduce the standard. For specific clauses or indicators, consult the official full-text system or a formally published copy.

When citing this standard, state both that it is withdrawn and which standard replaced it, so that readers do not mistake it for a current text. For products made after 2025-06-01, cite GB/T 10781.4-2024 first; only for historical products or old labels is it appropriate to cite GB/T 26760-2011, with its time context explained.

Finally, do not treat an aroma-type standard as a quality certification. Meeting a given edition of the Jiang-flavour baijiu standard means the product has reached that edition's consensus on terminology, sensory and physicochemical requirements; it does not mean it is necessarily better than other aroma types or other products. Quality judgement still returns to raw materials, process, storage, labelling and actual tasting.
