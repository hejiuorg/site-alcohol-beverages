Guizhou Dongjiu Co., Ltd.

Guizhou Dongjiu Co., Ltd. is based in Donggongsi town, Huichuan District, Zunyi, Guizhou, and is a company whose main business is baijiu. Dongjiu belongs to the dong-aroma type, also called medicinal aroma; production resumed in 1957, and in 1963 it joined China's old eight famous liquors. The company was registered in October 1994, and its current joint-stock name is one adopted after restructuring.

Company identity

This producer records the producer layer: company name, registered place, founding date and organisational form. Registry information gives the registered address as Yangfan Road, Donggongsi town, Huichuan District, Zunyi, and the registration date as 10 October 1994. Name, shareholders and registered capital change with restructuring, so this producer does not treat any single point in time as final.

When discussing "Dongjiu", first establish whether the subject is the joint-stock company, the distillery, the brand or a bottle. In company history, "resuming production", "winning at a liquor evaluation" and "restructuring" are events of different kinds and cannot be merged into one founding year.

Site and region

Donggongsi town lies north of central Zunyi in a humid subtropical monsoon climate, with mild winters and summers and relatively high humidity. Local water, sorghum and pit conditions suit a process that uses both large and small qu and long fermentation. Within the map of Guizhou's baijiu regions, Zunyi is a core area where sauce aroma and other aroma types coexist.

A site in Donggongsi does not mean every product is filled in the same workshop. Product lines, production bases and contract arrangements should be checked against the production address and the standard on the label.

Brands and products

Dongjiu is the core brand, and its products are mainly dong-aroma. Public product material lists series such as Baicaoxiang and Micang, with different positions and body designs; strength, volume, standards and prices change by year, so buyers should use the current label and official channels.

The dong-aroma style is often described as carrying herbal and medicinal notes, but "herbs in the qu" is a process description, not evidence that the product has health or therapeutic effects. Drink in moderation; any health-related claim should rest on content approved by regulators.

Craft and style

The key process at Dongjiu is the use of both large and small qu with dual-mash steam distillation: the large qu and small qu mashes are saccharified and fermented separately, the mash is distilled in two stages, and the spirit is drawn through the aromatic mash. Herbs are added during qu making to form distinctive aroma precursors; sorghum and water are the base materials, and the qu making and distillation steps together define the style.

This process differs from baijiu made with a single qu type and a single distillation, giving more complex aroma layers but also making production more dependent on experience. A process description states the style route only and cannot replace a judgement about a specific product; the standard and grade on the label are more worth checking.

History

In 1957 the local government decided to resume Dongjiu production, and trial production succeeded on the basis of the inherited process and recipe. In 1959 Dongjiu was rated a Guizhou famous liquor. In 1963 it was first rated a Chinese famous liquor at the second national liquor evaluation and entered the "old eight famous liquors" group. It continued to win awards at later national evaluations. In October 1994 the joint-stock company was registered; after later restructuring, production was restored and expanded. In August 2008 a dong-aroma baijiu local standard issued in Guizhou named Dongjiu as the representative of the type.

At the third, fourth and fifth national liquor evaluations in 1979, 1984 and 1989, Dongjiu again received national famous-liquor titles, a period that also saw capacity and exports expand. Craft detail belongs to this history too: the large qu uses wheat and the small qu uses rice, and the two are cultured separately; the mash and the aromatic mash are distilled in separate pots, and the spirit draws aroma as it passes through the aromatic mash during steam distillation; fermentation is mainly in pits, and herbs are added during qu making, with types and ratios in the company's technical scope. The company later restructured, adjusting craft transmission and capacity layout; product attribution and capacity figures should follow its current disclosure.

This producer does not provide unverified booking numbers, ticket prices or travel guides and does not recommend following old guides to the site.

Common misconceptions

First, treating the 1957 resumption as the joint-stock company's founding year. Second, treating "herbs in the qu" as a health benefit. A process description cannot replace regulatory approval. Third, equating dong-aroma with medicinal liquor. It is a baijiu aroma type, not a medicinal-liquor category. Fourth, treating the registered place as the origin of every product. Use the production address on the label. Fifth, treating the result of one liquor evaluation as today's product grade.
