Sichuan Langjiu Group Co., Ltd. is based in Erlang, Gulin County, Sichuan, on the left bank of the Chishui River. Public company-registry information shows that the group was registered in March 1998. Its core brand Langjiu is a sauce-aroma baijiu, covered in the Langjiu entry. Around 2002 the Baoguang group acquired control, and the company moved from state to private ownership.

Company identity

This producer records the producer layer: group name, registered place, founding date and organisational form. The group is the holding platform for the brand and assets, while production, sales and product specifications are handled by subsidiary companies; public material mentions several wholly owned and controlled subsidiaries, and the exact number changes with restructuring.

When discussing "Langjiu", first establish whether the subject is the group, the joint-stock company, the brand or a bottle. Registry entries, official profiles and financial reporting do not use identical definitions, so establish the entity first and then check which document a figure comes from.

Site and region

Erlang is in Gulin County, southern Sichuan, with the Chishui River running past the town. The Chishui valley is humid and hot in summer, and its terrain and microbial environment suit the open qu making and long fermentation of sauce-aroma baijiu. Langjiu summarises its site advantage as being born by the Chishui River, grown on Tianbao Peak, raised in the clay-jar warehouse and stored in Tianbao Cave, which correspond to brewing, qu making, storage and cave ageing.

Within the map of China's baijiu regions, Erlang and Maotai town in Guizhou share the Chishui sauce-aroma belt. The climate and craft traditions on the two banks are close, but company histories, product systems and standards are independent. A site in Erlang does not mean every product is filled in the same workshop, so the production address on the label still governs.

Brands and products

Langjiu is the group's core brand, with sauce-aroma baijiu as its main line. In public product material, Qinghua Lang is positioned as high-end sauce aroma and Honghua Lang covers the upper-mid price band; there are also strong-aroma and other product lines. Strength, volume, standards and prices change by year, so buyers should use the current label and official channels.

Brand narrative traces its origin to the late-Qing Xuzhi distillery and to earlier local records of "jujiang". Such narrative shows the link between the brand and the distilling tradition of the Chishui valley, but it is not the founding date of the modern company and cannot be converted into the age of the liquid in a bottle.

Craft and style

The basic sauce-aroma route is: sorghum as the raw material, qu made at the Dragon Boat Festival, grain added at the Double Ninth Festival, then qu making, high-temperature stacking, multi-round fermentation and fractionated distillation, followed by graded storage and blending. Langjiu's public description stresses high temperature, long fermentation and multi-round spirit drawing; base spirits are stored separately by round and grade and then blended across rounds and years.

Storage needs a separate note on this producer: the clay-jar warehouse is used for routine ageing, while natural caves such as Tianbao Cave and Dibao Cave are used for cave storage. Temperature and humidity in the caves are relatively stable, and the liquor changes slowly in clay jars. Cave storage is a method, not the exact age of the liquid in a bottle; the age and base-liquor ratio should be checked against company information.

History

In the late Qing, distilling workshops already existed around Erlang, and public accounts usually treat the Xuzhi distillery as the predecessor of the modern plant. In 1933 Huichuan Zaofang renamed "Huisha Langjiu" as "Langjiu". In 1957 production resumed locally and Langjiu entered its development phase in the People's Republic. In 1963 Langjiu won gold at the first Sichuan famous-liquor evaluation.

In March 1998 Sichuan Langjiu Group Co., Ltd. was registered. Between 2002 and 2003 the Baoguang group acquired control of Langjiu Group by taking over state-held equity, and the enterprise moved from state to private ownership. Media accounts differ slightly on the timing, consideration and equity ratio, so this producer uses only the points on which the reports agree.

Visiting

Langjiu Estate is on the left bank of the Chishui River at Erlang; public material states that it covers about 10 square kilometres and includes brewing, storage and cave-ageing stops. Whether the estate is open to the public, how visits are booked, whom it receives and what it charges all depend on the operator's current official notices. This producer does not provide unverified booking numbers or tour prices and does not recommend following old guides to the site.

Common misconceptions

First, treating the "jujiang" record as the founding year of Langjiu. A local-produce record and a company's management history are different things. Second, conflating the group, the joint-stock company and the brand. Their legal identities and disclosure duties differ. Third, treating cave storage as a guarantee of age. Cave storage is a method; age depends on the base-liquor composition and label information. Fourth, treating the Chishui region as a guarantee of quality. The region is only one condition; process execution and grade matter too. Fifth, treating the transaction details in one report as final. Timing and consideration differ across media accounts.

For Sichuan Langjiu Group Co., Ltd., begin with the range it makes, then the site and the owner, and finish with the bottler named on the label; that order keeps the account grounded.
