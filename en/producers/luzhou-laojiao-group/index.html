Luzhou Laojiao Group Co., Ltd.

A wholly state-owned enterprise group in Luzhou, Sichuan, founded in December 2000 and ultimately controlled by the Luzhou municipal SASAC; as of the end of the third quarter of 2026 it held 26.05% of Luzhou Laojiao Co., Ltd. (stock code 000568), whose controlling shareholder it is.

Luzhou Laojiao Group Co., Ltd. is a textbook example of the two-tier "group plus listed company" structure in baijiu: the group was founded in December 2000 as a wholly state-owned enterprise, while Luzhou Laojiao Co., Ltd. was listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange in 1994 under stock code 000568. The names are nearly identical, but the legal identities, financial scope and external responsibilities are entirely different. Reading the company therefore starts not with the product but with the layer being discussed.

Group identity

The group is registered and mainly operates in Luzhou, Sichuan, and its enterprise type is a wholly state-owned company. The group leans towards resource integration, industrial investment and support businesses; the listed company handles baijiu production, sales and disclosure. The group is not the distillery, and the listed company is not the whole of the group's assets.

This page records producer identity. It does not evaluate flavour and does not reproduce promotional copy. Corporate history, ownership, capacity and disputes are checkable information; brand stories and tasting notes belong to another language, and mixing them lets narrative pass as fact.

Ownership and ultimate controller

The ultimate controller is the Luzhou municipal State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission (SASAC). SASAC holds listed-company shares through the group, creating a "SASAC, group, listed company" control chain. Each link has its own legal identity and cannot be merged into one subject.

At the end of the third quarter of 2026 the group held 26.05% of Luzhou Laojiao Co., Ltd. and was its controlling shareholder. Percentages change with placements, buy-backs and state-equity adjustments, so any citation should state the reporting date and data source rather than use an old figure for the current structure.

Relationship with the listed company

The listed company produces and sells baijiu and discloses quarterly reports, annual reports and ad hoc announcements under regulatory requirements. Revenue, profit, capacity, inventory and share price usually belong to the listed-company scope; industrial investment, packaging, logistics and other support businesses at group level are not in those accounts.

The question "how much revenue does Luzhou Laojiao have" therefore needs a prior answer: the listed company or the group. Treating listed-company data as group-wide, or group investment income as baijiu revenue, produces a scope error. This is the most common confusion in public discussion.

The 2009 equity transfer

Public information shows that in September 2009 the Luzhou municipal SASAC transferred its Luzhou Laojiao shares to the group without consideration, creating a parent-subsidiary structure. The aim was to clarify the management levels of state capital, with the group as holding platform and the listed company focused on operations.

The transfer was not an asset restructuring and did not change production. What changed was the shareholder register and the control path. This explains why the group, founded in 2000, is still the controlling shareholder of a company listed in 1994.

Luzhou production area and pits

Luzhou lies in southeastern Sichuan, where the Yangtze and Tuo rivers meet, with a warm humid climate suited to solid-state mud-pit fermentation. Strong-aroma baijiu depends on continuous use of pits: the microbial community in the pit mud stabilises with age and the ecology of aroma-producing bacteria matures. Within the wider map of China's baijiu regions, Luzhou is an important strong-aroma node.

The Luzhou Laojiao pit group is an important physical asset of the region. Public information states that the pits begun in 1573 were listed as a Major Historical and Cultural Site Protected at the National Level in 1996. Two things must be kept apart: the pit age has heritage and archival support, while "1573" is also a product name, and a brand name is not the age of the liquid.

Business segments

The group's main business is alcohol, with support operations in packaging, logistics and financial investment. The support businesses lower transaction costs for the main business and generate diversified returns for local state capital, but they do not face consumers directly and do not enter a baijiu product's cost label.

Segment boundaries change by year. The group may create or exit investment platforms, while the listed company keeps a relatively stable production system. When reading group information, the useful question is which businesses sit inside the listed company and which outside, rather than counting every affiliate as a distillery.

Brand and product layers

At brand level there are positions such as "Luzhou Laojiao" and "Guojiao 1573"; at product level these split further by strength, volume and series, with strong aroma as the main line. The brand answers what it is called, the company answers who makes it, and the product answers what is in this bottle.

Mixing the three layers produces two common misreadings: taking company history as the age of a particular bottle, and taking brand position as a quality grade. Both should be checked against the label and public disclosure. Product details sit in the Luzhou Laojiao entry and are not repeated here.

Capacity and storage

Baijiu capacity is set jointly by the number of pits, brewing workshops, qu-making capacity and packaging lines, while storage depends on clay-jar warehouses, liquor stores and cave facilities. Strong-aroma baijiu values continuous pit use, and pit age shapes base-liquor style, but an old pit does not make every bottle old.

Published capacity is usually design capacity; actual output depends on raw materials, season and orders. Storage is the basis for base-liquor maturation, not finished-goods inventory. Treating storage as sellable stock, or pit count as annual output, are common scope errors.

Channels and market

Luzhou Laojiao sells through distributors, specialist stores, supermarkets, restaurants and e-commerce, across a price range from mass to premium. Premium products carry brand image, while mid- and low-priced products carry volume and distribution. More channel layers make price discipline harder, and cross-region dumping and price inversion are industry-wide problems.

Market position should be read from third-party data and company disclosure, not advertising spend alone. Baijiu is concentrated, and competition among leading firms plays out in channels, terminals and consumer perception; one year's revenue ranking cannot establish a long-term structure.

Governance and related-party transactions

As a state-controlled listed company, Luzhou Laojiao must disclose related-party transactions, guarantees and major investments under securities rules. Trademark licensing, purchases, sales and fund flows between the group and the listed company are set out with pricing basis when they reach disclosure thresholds.

Governance is about boundaries: the group exercises shareholder rights, while the listed company's board and management run the business. Related-party transactions need pricing evidence and independent-director opinions; otherwise minority shareholders may suspect tunnelling.

Disputes and risks

The first is the meaning of "1573": it is both a statement about when the pits were begun and the name of a premium product, and consumers easily read the product name as age. The second is conflation of group and listed company, which media and social accounts frequently mix. The third is channel inventory and price inversion, especially visible in premium baijiu during demand adjustments. The fourth is brand licensing and sub-brand management, where excessive licensing dilutes the main brand.

These risks are not a verdict on the company; they are checkpoints when reading reports and news. Turning a risk into a conclusion, or promotional copy into fact, does not suit an encyclopedic entry.

Division of labour with the product page

This page handles the group and listed company: who holds shares, who controls, where the business boundary lies and which scope a figure belongs to. The product page handles a specific bottle: aroma type, strength, volume, process and flavour. Both say "Luzhou Laojiao", but one answers who owns and who operates, the other what is in this bottle. Putting equity structure on the product page, or tasting language on the group page, makes readers look in the wrong place.

Common misconceptions

First, conflating the group with the joint-stock company. Their legal identities and disclosure duties differ. Second, treating "1573" as the age of the liquid. It is first of all a pit and product name. Third, treating the registered place as the origin of every product. Use the production address on the label. Fourth, treating design capacity as actual output. Utilisation depends on the year and orders. Fifth, treating a protected heritage site as an ordinary production workshop. Conservation and production rules are not the same.
