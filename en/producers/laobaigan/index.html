Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Liquor Co., Ltd. is a Laobaigan-aroma baijiu producer in Hengshui, Hebei. The company originates from the state-run Hengshui distillery formed in 1946 when the government took over 18 traditional private workshops and placed them under state ownership. It listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange on 29 October 2002. Its brands include Hengshui Laobaigan, Bancheng Shaoguo, Wuling and Wenwang Gongjiu.

Company identity

This page records the producer layer: company name, registered place, founding date and organisational form. The joint-stock company is the listed entity and handles production, sales and disclosure; the brand is a market mark, and a product is a specific bottle. When discussing "Laobaigan", first establish whether the subject is the aroma type, the brand or a bottle.

For Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Liquor Co., Ltd., "Laobaigan" is at once an aroma-type name, a brand name and a product name. Aroma type is a classification of process and flavour, a brand is a market mark, and a product is a specific specification; the three are not interchangeable.

Site and region

Hengshui lies in south-eastern Hebei, in the Heilonggang river basin, with a warm-temperate semi-humid monsoon climate, distinct seasons and concentrated rainfall. Company material places the production base in Hengshui and traces brewing history back more than 1900 years, stressing a local narrative of rising in the Han, flourishing in the Tang and taking its name in the Ming.

Regional conditions affect raw materials and the microbial environment but do not determine quality by themselves. Laobaigan-aroma flavour also depends on raw materials, qu, fermentation in earthen jars or pits, distillation and storage. The raw materials and origin of a specific product should be taken from the label.

A site in Hengshui does not mean every product uses the same workshop or the same batch of base liquor. The company also owns Bancheng Shaoguo, Wuling and Wenwang Gongjiu, which sit in different regions and use different aroma types and processes.

Brands and products

The core brand is Hengshui Laobaigan, with public product material covering mass to mid-to-high price bands. The company also owns Chengde Qianlongzui (Bancheng Shaoguo), Wuling and Wenwang Gongjiu, each in a different region and with a different aroma type and process.

Strength, volume, packaging and price change by year and channel, and some products have regional or commemorative versions. Buyers should check the brand, product name, strength, net content, ingredients, producer and standard on the label rather than the three characters of the name alone.

For Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Liquor Co., Ltd., the brand-to-product relation is one-to-many: one brand has several series and price points, and a price band is market positioning, not a quality grade.

Craft and style

The typical Laobaigan-aroma route uses sorghum and other grains, medium- or low-temperature qu, fermentation in earthen jars or pits, solid-state distillation, fractional collection, storage and blending. Compared with strong aroma, Laobaigan stresses a balance of clarity, mellow body and clean finish; compared with light aroma, its fermentation vessels and qu combination differ.

"Laobaigan" was originally a colloquial term for a strong, dry baijiu and later became an aroma-type and brand name. An aroma-type name states a style direction and cannot be converted directly into strength, ingredients or a quality grade.

A process description states a style route only and cannot replace a judgement about a specific product. Products under different brands and series within the same company may differ in raw materials, qu, fermentation and storage.

History

For Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Liquor Co., Ltd., in 1946 the government took over 18 traditional private workshops in Hengshui and formed the Ji'nan Administrative Office State-run Hengshui Distillery, the direct predecessor of the company. On 29 October 2002 Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Liquor Co., Ltd. listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange.

After listing the company formed a multi-brand structure through acquisition and integration, including Bancheng Shaoguo, Wuling and Wenwang Gongjiu. A multi-brand structure means different aroma types, regions and price bands coexist, and one brand's flavour cannot describe the whole company.

Common misconceptions

First, treating "Laobaigan" only as a brand. It is also an aroma-type name and a product name. Second, treating 1946 as the year the joint-stock company was founded. 1946 is the year the state-run distillery was formed. Third, treating a brewing tradition of more than 1900 years as the length of the modern company's history. Regional tradition and corporate history are different scales. Fourth, treating the group's multiple brands as one aroma type. Bancheng Shaoguo, Wuling and Wenwang Gongjiu differ in aroma type and region. Fifth, treating price as a quality grade. Price is market positioning, not a process standard.

Hengshui Laobaigan is a listed baijiu company rooted in the former state-run Hengshui distillery. Its portfolio also includes Bancheng, Wuling and Wenwang brands acquired over time. The parent, the listed company, the brands and the individual bottles are different layers. Acquired brands should not automatically be described as laobaigan aroma simply because they belong to the same corporate group.
