VarietiesReviewedEN

Aglianico

A late-ripening red grape of southern Italy with deep colour and high tannin and acidity, best known from Taurasi in Campania and the volcanic Vulture zone in Basilicata, and built for long ageing.

On this page · 13Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Albariño

A white grape of Spain's Atlantic northwest, known as Alvarinho in Portugal, with thick skins, high acidity and aromas of citrus, white flowers and sea air, centred on Rías Baixas and Monção e Melgaço.

On this page · 11Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Assyrtiko

A white grape from Santorini in Greece that keeps high acidity in wind, drought and volcanic soil, giving dry wines of lemon, flint and sea salt, with Vinsanto as its sweet counterpart.

On this page · 13Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Barbera

A red grape of Piedmont in Italy with high acidity, medium tannin and deep colour, heat-tolerant and steady in yield, often contrasted with Nebbiolo as high acid against high tannin. Barbera d'Asti became a DOCG in 2008 with a minimum of 90% Barbera.

Blaufränkisch

A red grape of Burgenland, Austria, also called Kékfrankos in Hungary and Frankovka in Czechia; deep in colour with black cherry, pepper and spice, high acidity, medium-high tannin and good ageing potential.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Cabernet Franc

A red grape variety from Bordeaux and the Loire Valley in France, with thinner skins than Cabernet Sauvignon and earlier ripening, moderate tannin and expressive aroma, known for raspberry, violet, graphite and green pepper notes. It is one of the parents of Cabernet Sauvignon and is used both as a varietal wine and in blends on Bordeaux's Right Bank and in the Loire.

Carignan

A western Mediterranean red grape from Aragon in Spain, widely planted in Languedoc, Catalonia and Sardinia; old low-yielding vines give high acidity, firm tannin, spice and earth.

Carménère

A red grape from Bordeaux that was long mistaken for Merlot in Chile, where it was identified again in 1994 and became the country's signature variety, with deep colour, late ripening and notes of black plum, blackberry and green pepper.

Chenin Blanc

A white grape native to the Loire Valley, thin-skinned, high in acidity and late-ripening, prone to botrytis and able to make dry, off-dry, sweet and sparkling wine. It shows quince, apple, honey and chamomile, and DNA work indicates it is a cross of Savagnin and an unknown variety; in South Africa it is called Steen.

Cinsault

A southern French red grape, drought-tolerant and high-yielding, light in colour, low in tannin and medium-high in acidity; used in rosé, blends and the Southern Rhône’s Cinsault-Grenache combination, and planted in South Africa and Lebanon.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Corvina

A red grape variety from Veneto, Italy, with relatively thin skins and moderate colour, known for sour cherry, almond and herbal aromas. It is the core variety of Valpolicella and Amarone, usually blended with Corvinone and Rondinella.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Dolcetto

A red grape of Piedmont, Italy, whose name means “little sweet”, though the wine is usually dry with low acidity and rounded, gently extracted tannin; black cherry, plum, liquorice and an almond finish, drunk young as an everyday contrast to Nebbiolo and Barbera.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Fetească Neagră

A native red grape of Romania and Moldova, whose name means “black girl”; deep in colour with medium-high tannin, black cherry, plum, spice and earth, often compared to a mix of Pinot Noir and Syrah.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Furmint

Furmint is the white grape behind Tokaj, high in acidity with thick skins and a strong affinity for noble rot, producing both botrytised sweet wines such as Aszú and a growing range of dry whites with citrus, smoke and saline mineral character.

On this page · 11Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Gamay

A thin-skinned, early-ripening red grape with high acidity and the core variety of Beaujolais reds; known for whole-cluster fermentation and fruit from granite soils, with styles from light and easy to ageworthy village crus.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Garganega

A white grape of Soave in Veneto, Italy, late-ripening and high-yielding, with lemon, almond, white flowers and mineral; it makes dry Soave, sweet Recioto di Soave and sparkling wine.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Gewürztraminer

An aromatic white grape with pink skins, named after Tramin in South Tyrol. An aromatic mutation of Savagnin, it is low in acidity and quick to accumulate sugar, known for lychee, rose, ginger and cinnamon, and grown mainly in Alsace, Germany, Austria and northern Italy.

On this page · 12Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Glera

A white grape of Veneto and Friuli in Italy, known as Prosecco for both grape and wine until 2009, when the variety was renamed Glera to protect the place name. Thin-skinned with moderate acidity, it shows green apple, pear and white flowers and is the core of Prosecco, called Serprino around the Euganean Hills.

On this page · 11Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Godello

A white grape of Valdeorras, Ribeiro and Monterrei in northwest Spain, with citrus, white flowers, mineral and nuts, medium-high acidity and a full texture; it can be made crisp or oaked.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Grenache

A heat- and drought-tolerant red grape with thin skins, fast sugar accumulation and low acidity, supporting fruit and alcohol in Rhône blends and in Spanish Priorat. DNA work confirms that Spain's Garnacha and Sardinia's Cannonau are the same variety.

On this page · 12Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Grüner Veltliner

Austria's signature white grape, late-ripening and high in acidity, with citrus, green pea and white pepper aromas. DNA work shows it is a natural cross of Traminer and the old Austrian variety St. Georgen, and in the Wachau it is graded by alcohol into Steinfeder, Federspiel and Smaragd.

On this page · 11Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Kadarka

A red grape of Hungary and the Balkans, pale in colour, high in acidity and low in tannin, with red cherry, spice and pepper; once Hungary’s most important red grape, it survives as a traditional variety in Szekszárd and Eger.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Macabeo

A white grape of northern Spain, called Viura in Rioja and Macabeo in Catalonia; medium acidity with green apple, white flowers and citrus, it is the base of Cava and Rioja whites.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Malbec

A red grape native to south-west France with deep-coloured skins and generous tannin, sensitive to frost and poor fruit set; it became a flagship variety in the high-altitude vineyards of Mendoza, Argentina.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Malvasia

A white variety family across the Mediterranean and Iberia, with many clones and types; aromas range from white flowers, apricot and honey to spice, and it makes dry, sweet and fortified wines, so it is not one single variety.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Marsanne

A white grape of the Northern Rhône, usually blended with Roussanne; full-bodied, low in acidity, with honey, beeswax, nuts and white flowers, rounder with age, and the main variety of Hermitage white.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Melon de Bourgogne

A white grape native to Burgundy, now grown mainly around Nantes in the Loire Valley for Muscadet. A cross of Gouais Blanc and Pinot like Chardonnay, it is high in acidity and neutral in aroma, and often gains roundness from lees ageing.

On this page · 11Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Mencía

A red grape of Bierzo and Ribeira Sacra in northwest Spain, with black cherry, herbs, spice and slate mineral, bright acidity and fine moderate tannin; historically mistaken for Cabernet Franc, sometimes compared with Pinot Noir or Gamay.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Merlot

A red grape common on Bordeaux's Right Bank, with medium berries, relatively thin skins and early ripening; its tannin and acidity are moderate, and it is used both as a varietal wine and in blends with Cabernet Sauvignon.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Montepulciano

A red grape of Abruzzo, Italy, unrelated to the Tuscan town of Montepulciano; deep in colour with medium-low tannin, medium-high acidity, black cherry, plum and spice, it is one of southern Italy’s most common red wines.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Mourvèdre

A late-ripening red grape from Spain and the Mediterranean coast with thick skins, firm tannin and good heat and drought tolerance; in Provence and the Rhône it is usually blended with Grenache and Syrah.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Müller-Thurgau

A German-bred white grape, a cross of Riesling and Madeleine Royale; early-ripening and high-yielding, with flowers, citrus and a light Muscat note, it is one of Germany’s most planted whites.

On this page · 9Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Muscat

Muscat is not one grape but a large, ancient family whose members share a striking floral and grapey aroma, ranging from dry Alsace white and lightly sparkling Italian Moscato to French and Australian fortified sweet wines.

On this page · 11Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Nebbiolo

The signature red grape of Piedmont in Italy; it ripens late and has thick skins, giving wines that are pale in colour yet high in acidity and tannin, with aromas of rose, tar and red cherry. Barolo and Barbaresco are built on it.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Nero d’Avola

Sicily’s most important red grape, deep in colour with medium-high tannin, blackberry, black cherry, plum and spice; it performs differently around Noto, Vittoria and Etna and is often blended with Frappato.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

País

A Chilean old-vine red grape descended from Spain’s Listán Negro/Gros Collet, preserved in dry old vineyards of Itata and Maule; light in colour, high in acidity, low in tannin, with red cherry, dried herbs and spice.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Palomino

A white grape of Andalusia and the main variety of Sherry, neutral and low in acidity, whose style comes from biological ageing under flor or oxidative ageing in a solera.

On this page · 13Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Parellada

A white grape of Penedès, Catalonia, one of Cava’s three main varieties; high in acidity, light in body, with green apple, citrus and white flowers, often blended with Xarel·lo and Macabeo for freshness.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Petit Manseng

A white grape of Jurançon in southwest France, with small thick-skinned berries, late ripening, tropical fruit, citrus, honey and high acidity; it makes sweet, off-dry and dry wines and is one of France’s longest-lived whites.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Petit Verdot

A minority red grape in Bordeaux blends, late-ripening with thick skins, deep colour and firm tannin; it struggles to ripen fully in cool Bordeaux but is used in Australia, Spain and the US to add structure and colour.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Pinot Blanc

Pinot Blanc is a white mutation of Pinot Noir with moderate to high acidity and a gentle, rounded style, grown in Alsace (often blended with Auxerrois), Germany as Weissburgunder and northern Italy as Pinot Bianco, for still whites and sparkling wine.

Pinot Gris

A grey-skinned colour mutation of Pinot Noir, early-ripening, thin-skinned and low in acidity. Alsace can make it full and rich, while Italy makes it light and crisp as Pinot Grigio; the Italian delle Venezie DOC, created in 2017, expanded that style further.

Pinot Meunier

Pinot Meunier is the dark-skinned Pinot mutation that buds and ripens early, performs well on the clay slopes of the Marne Valley and gives Champagne red-fruit richness and roundness, whether in blends or in increasingly popular single-varietal and Blanc de Noirs bottlings.

Pinotage

A South African red grape created in 1925 by crossing Pinot Noir with Cinsaut at Stellenbosch, known for deep colour, firm tannin and a style that ranges from fresh fruit to coffee and smoke.

Piquepoul

A white grape of Languedoc, France, centred on Picpoul de Pinet; high acidity, lemon, sea salt and white flowers, it suits oysters and seafood and represents the Mediterranean “sea-air” white.

Plavac Mali

A red grape of the Dalmatian coast, Croatia, a cross of Tribidrag (Zinfandel) and Dobričić; high in tannin and alcohol, with blackberry, dried fruit and spice, with Dingač as the core appellation.

Riesling

A high-acid, clearly aromatic white grape known for lime, green apple and, with age, mineral and petrol notes; Germany, Alsace and Austria are core regions, and sweetness ranges widely.

Rkatsiteli

One of Georgia’s oldest white grapes, high in acidity with green apple, white flowers and spice; it makes crisp dry whites and amber wines through qvevri skin contact, and is central to Georgia’s clay-vessel tradition.

Roussanne

A white grape of the Northern Rhône, with white flowers, almond, honey and herbal notes, higher acidity and lower yields than Marsanne; usually blended with Marsanne, and also planted in Savoie and Languedoc.

Sangiovese

Tuscany's signature red grape, with high acidity and medium tannin and notes of sour cherry, tomato leaf and earth; it is the core variety of Chianti and Brunello.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Saperavi

A red grape from Kakheti, Georgia, known for the deep colour and high acidity that come from its red-fleshed (teinturier) skins; it makes dry, semi-sweet, sweet and fortified wines and is Georgia’s national wine symbol.

Sauvignon Blanc

An aromatic white grape known for citrus, grass, gooseberry and tropical notes; it has high acidity and suits cool regions, showing different faces in the Loire, Bordeaux and New Zealand.

Sémillon

A Bordeaux white grape with thin skins and low acidity, prone to botrytis, the main variety of Sauternes and Barsac and also used for dry Graves white. It shows lemon, beeswax, honey and apricot, and in the 1855 classification Château d'Yquem was the only estate rated Premier Cru Supérieur.

St. Laurent

A Central European red grape in the Pinot family; deep in colour with bright acidity and medium-plus tannin, firm young and velvety with age, black cherry, blackberry, spice and earth, between Pinot Noir and Blaufränkisch in style.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Sylvaner

A Central European white grape planted in Alsace, Germany and Austria; medium-bodied with medium-high acidity, green apple, white flowers, herbs and mineral, often underrated.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Syrah

A deeply coloured red grape with generous tannin and notes of black pepper, blackberry and violet; it originated in the Rhône Valley and is widely planted in Australia under the name Shiraz.

Tannat

A red variety from Madiran in southwest France, known for high tannin, deep colour and long ageing; in Uruguay it became a national signature, often blended with Merlot or Cabernet Franc to soften structure.

Tempranillo

Spain's signature red grape, with relatively thick skins and early ripening and notes of cherry, leather and tobacco; Rioja and Ribera del Duero often use oak ageing to shape its structure.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Torrontés

A native Argentine white grape, known for rose, citrus, lychee and spice; high-altitude Salta versions have higher acidity and more concentrated perfume, and it is often mistaken for the Muscat family.

Trebbiano

A high-acid, low-alcohol white grape widely grown in Italy as Trebbiano and in France as Ugni Blanc, where it is the base of Cognac and Armagnac.

Trousseau

A red grape of the Jura, France, pale in colour, high in acidity and low in tannin, with red cherry, spice and earth; it makes light reds, rosés and sparkling wine and is central to the Jura’s light-body tradition.

Verdejo

A white grape of Rueda in Spain, with fennel, citrus, white flowers and herbal notes and crisp acidity; often blended with Sauvignon Blanc or Viognier, it is the core of modern Spanish white wine.

Vermentino

A Mediterranean coastal white grape planted in Sardinia, Liguria, Tuscany and Provence (as Rolle); citrus, white flowers, sea salt and herbs, medium-high acidity, suited to seafood.

Viognier

A white grape native to the Northern Rhône, with thicker skins, low acidity and intense aroma. In Condrieu it gives full-bodied apricot and honeysuckle wines, and it is often blended in small amounts with Syrah for aroma and colour stability. It nearly disappeared in the mid-20th century, with only about 30 ha left in Condrieu around 1965.

Xarel·lo

A core white grape of Penedès and Cava sparkling wine in Spain, high in acidity with green apple, citrus, white flowers and mineral; with Macabeo and Parellada it forms the classic Cava trio.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Xinomavro

A red grape from Naoussa in northern Greece, known for high acidity, firm tannin and tomato-olive notes, often called “the Greek Nebbiolo”; with age it gains leather, dried fruit and spice.

On this page · 8Read VarietiesReviewedEN

Zinfandel

A red grape native to Dalmatia in Croatia and genetically identical to Italy's Primitivo, brought to the United States in the 19th century and now the signature grape of California old vines and high-alcohol reds.

Zweigelt

Austria’s most important red grape, a cross of Blaufränkisch and St. Laurent; early-ripening and high-yielding, deep in colour with low tannin, black cherry, spice and violet, best drunk young.
